These dynamical signatures align with the pseudokinase paradigm, wherein ATP binding often serves regulatory rather than catalytic purposes [2]. The limited pocket flexibility and preserved nucleotide positioning are reminiscent of ATP‑regulated chaperones and ion channels, where ligand occupancy stabilizes a specific functional state [5]. The observed low inter‑lobe correlation further supports a decoupled lobe architecture, a feature reported for other pseudokinases that lack the canonical activation‑loop phosphorylation machinery [1,3]. Thus, the MD data corroborate the notion that KAPCA’s ATP binding pocket is structurally competent yet dynamically restrained, potentially reflecting a regulatory role rather than enzymatic activity.

In conclusion, KAPCA maintains a stable ATP binding mode over 200 ns, with minimal pocket fluctuations and a distinct dynamical signature compared to its active counterparts. Follow‑up investigations could target the flexible αC‑β4 loop through site‑directed mutagenesis to assess its contribution to ligand affinity and allosteric signalling, while ^15N‑NMR relaxation experiments could validate the simulated Cα flexibility profile.
