Pseudokinases have emerged as a distinct subclass of protein kinases that retain an ATP‑binding pocket yet lack catalytic competence or display non‑canonical catalytic mechanisms. Recent reviews of allosteric regulation emphasize that even in the absence of phosphotransfer, ATP binding can propagate conformational signals that modulate protein–protein interactions or the stability of regulatory domains [2]. In the context of our 200‑ns MD trajectories for the five holo structures, the persistence of a well‑defined ATP pocket in KAPCA and the pseudokinase KSR1 contrasts with the more flexible binding sites observed in CDK2 and ILK, mirroring the heterogeneous conformational ensembles reported for other ATP‑binding proteins such as Hsp70 chaperones, where ATP binding stabilizes the closed state but permits subtle loop reorientations [3].

The suite of scalar descriptors we extracted—ATP center‑of‑mass (COM) distances, orientation angles relative to a pocket axis, side‑chain χ₁ circular statistics, and dihedral‑based PCA scalars—mirrors the quantitative metrics routinely used to characterize ligand‑induced motions in kinases and transporters. For instance, the mean and standard deviation of ATP COM distances provide an analogue to the “ATP‑binding pocket closure” measurements routinely derived from NMR and cryo‑EM studies of the ABC transporter family, where the degree of pocket compaction correlates with ATP hydrolysis rates [8]. Likewise, the χ₁ circular mean and spread capture side‑chain rotameric flexibility that underlies the “activation loop” dynamics in protein kinases, a feature highlighted in the crystal–structure analysis of JAK2 and its pseudokinase counterpart KSR1. The DCCM mean correlation between N‑ and C‑lobes is analogous to the inter‑domain correlation matrices reported for the Src family kinases, where a tighter correlation is associated with active conformations.

When these descriptors were assembled into a feature table and subjected to Ward hierarchical clustering, the resulting dendrogram revealed a clear partitioning of the five systems into two major branches, consistent with the functional dichotomy between active kinases (CDK2, JAK2, ILK) and pseudokinases (KAPCA, KSR1). A k = 4 cut isolates KSR1 as a distinct outlier, in line with experimental evidence that pseudokinases can act as scaffolds rather than enzymes and thus display markedly different dynamics from canonical kinases [2]. The heat‑map of z‑scored descriptors further demonstrates that the ATP‑COM distance and side‑chain χ₁ spread are the most discriminating features, reinforcing the notion that ATP‑binding pocket geometry is a primary determinant of pseudokinase function. Collectively, these computational findings resonate with the mechanistic insights from recent experimental studies on ATP‑dependent allostery in non‑catalytic proteins and provide a quantitative framework for comparing the dynamic landscapes of active versus pseudokinase ATP‑holo states.
