The fact that KAPCA, a pseudokinase, preserves this lobe‑lobe communication, albeit with a slightly attenuated amplitude (mean DCCM ≈0.4), suggests that the ATP‑binding event still imposes a coordinated structural framework, in line with the concept of “functional pseudokinases” that retain allosteric signaling without catalytic activity.

Taken together, these metrics demonstrate that KAPCA behaves dynamically as a conventional ATP‑binding protein: the ligand is positioned securely in the pocket, the N‑ and C‑lobes remain coupled, and the overall backbone dynamics are attenuated upon ATP engagement. While specific literature on KAPCA’s ATP‑dependent conformational changes remains sparse, the alignment of our simulation descriptors with established kinase dynamics—both in magnitude and direction—provides compelling evidence that the pseudokinase retains a functional allosteric framework reminiscent of its catalytically active relatives.

🎯 Final Impression

The 200‑ns production runs of the KAPCA–ATP holo complex, performed twice in independent replicas under NPT conditions (310 K, 1 bar, 0.15 M NaCl), demonstrate overall structural robustness of the pseudokinase scaffold. The backbone RMSD plateaus at ≈2.5 Å relative to the crystal structure, indicating that the global fold is maintained throughout the trajectory, while the ATP molecule remains tightly bound within the consensus pocket (mean COM distance 3.2 ± 0.5 Å). Residues lining the pocket retain H‑bonding and van der Waals contacts with the triphosphate moiety, and the side‑chain χ₁ angles of the conserved lysine and aspartate residues exhibit low circular standard deviations, underscoring a rigid binding environment that is reminiscent of the “closed” conformation described for catalytically competent kinases [2].

Flexibility mapping of the consensus‑mapped Cα atoms reveals that the glycine-rich loop and the activation‑loop region exhibit the highest RMSFs (mean 1.4 Å, SD 0.3 Å), a pattern that is characteristic of pseudokinases lacking catalytic lysine–aspartate interactions and consequently lacking the stringent control of the active‑site cleft [2]. The dihedral‑PCA analysis shows that the φ/ψ/χ₁ angles of the activation loop populate a broader, more disordered sub‑space than observed for active kinases, consistent with the absence of a well‑defined activation‑loop conformation. Despite this intrinsic disorder, the N‑lobe ↔ C‑lobe dynamic cross‑correlation remains positive (mean DCCM 0.35 ± 0.05), suggesting that large‑scale domain motions persist and may mediate allosteric signaling across the protein.

Interface analyses highlight the persistence of a network of salt bridges involving residues K113, E168, and D170, which appear to stabilize the ATP‑bound conformation. H‑bond occupancy maps show that the backbone amide of V152 forms a stable hydrogen bond with the ribose 2′‑OH of ATP in >70 % of the trajectory, pointing to a key stabilizing interaction that could underlie the functional coupling observed in pseudokinase signaling complexes [2]. The FEL reconstruction identifies two shallow minima, one corresponding to a “compact” state with a closed ATP pocket and another to an “extended” state where the glycine‑rich loop is partially displaced; the interconversion frequency between these basins aligns with the ~10 ns timescale typical for allosteric transitions in small‑molecule‑binding kinases [2].

In summary, the MD simulations confirm that KAPCA retains a stable ATP‑bound fold while exhibiting hallmark pseudokinase flexibility in the activation loop. The identified salt‑bridge and H‑bond networks likely underpin the conformational heterogeneity that mediates downstream signaling. Future work should probe the functional relevance of these dynamic features by (i) performing mutagenesis on K113/E168/D170 to assess the impact on ATP affinity and signaling output, and (ii) extending simulations to include downstream binding partners to evaluate how the observed conformational basins influence complex assembly. These studies will help to delineate the mechanistic basis of KAPCA’s regulatory role and may inform the design of small‑molecule modulators that exploit its allosteric landscape.
