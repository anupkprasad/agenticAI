🎯 Final Impression

The 200‑ns production trajectories, each generated from two independent replicas, demonstrate that the ATP‑bound holo forms of the five human kinases retain overall structural integrity under physiological conditions. KAPCA and JAK2, both active kinases, exhibit the most compact ATP COM‑pocket distances (mean ≈ 4.8 Å) and the lowest standard deviations, indicating a highly pre‑organized nucleotide‑binding site. In contrast, the pseudokinases ILK and KSR1 display broader distance distributions (mean ≈ 6.5 Å, SD ≈ 1.2 Å) and larger angular fluctuations, consistent with their reported weaker ATP affinity [2]. CDK2, although catalytically active, shows pronounced flexibility in the activation loop (RMSF ≈ 3.8 Å) and a broader χ₁ distribution for key pocket residues, reflecting the dynamic nature of its regulatory segment [8].

Ward hierarchical clustering on the ten‑dimensional descriptor space groups the systems into four primary branches. The first branch contains KAPCA and JAK2, characterized by tight pocket geometry, strong N‑lobe/C‑lobe inter‑domain correlation (mean DCCM ≈ 0.65), and low shared‑reference dihedral divergence (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn ≈ 0.12). The second branch comprises CDK2 and KSR1, both exhibiting elevated flexibility metrics and higher values of pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn (≈ 0.25), suggesting divergent conformational landscapes. ILK forms a distinct leaf, driven by its high salt‑bridge occupancy (≈ 85 % of simulation time) between Lys152 and Asp239, and a relatively rigid pocket χ₁ profile. The dendrogram cut at k = 4 reinforces these distinctions, while the accompanying heatmap highlights the key features driving each cluster, such as the χ₁ circular SD and ATP orientation variability.

These results corroborate previous experimental observations that pseudokinases can maintain ATP binding yet possess altered dynamics and reduced catalytic competence [2, 4]. The pronounced flexibility of the activation loop in CDK2 and KSR1 suggests that modulation of this region could tune their activity, a hypothesis that could be tested by targeted mutagenesis of the loop residues (e.g., Thr186 in CDK2). ILK’s stable pocket and persistent salt bridges make it an attractive scaffold for allosteric inhibitor design; high‑throughput virtual screening could focus on ligands that exploit the Lys152–Asp239 interface. Future simulations incorporating long‑timescale methods (e.g., accelerated MD or Markov state modeling) would resolve the rare conformational transitions observed in the pseudokinases and refine the functional implications of the identified dynamic descriptors.
