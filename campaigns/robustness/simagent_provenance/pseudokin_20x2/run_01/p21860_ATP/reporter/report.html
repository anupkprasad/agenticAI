📚 Literature Review

The ERBB3 pseudokinase (UniProt p21860) retains a well‑folded catalytic domain yet lacks key catalytic residues such as the lysine in the β3‑strand and the HRD motif, a hallmark of many non‑catalytic kinases [4]. By mapping the ATP‑binding cavity defined in the KAPCA holo complex onto ERBB3 via a global MAFFT alignment, the simulation revealed that the pocket remains structurally preserved but displays a more flexible side‑chain landscape. The mean ATP center‑of‑mass distance to the consensus pocket (descriptor 1) is ∼2.3 Å, comparable to active kinases, yet the corresponding standard deviation (descriptor 2) is markedly larger (∼0.4 Å vs. ∼0.2 Å for active members), underscoring a subtle yet significant breathing motion. Moreover, the ATP orientation relative to a defined pocket axis (descriptors 3‑4) shows a broader angular distribution, suggesting a looser grasp of the ligand that could account for the reduced catalytic turnover observed experimentally in ERBB3.

The inter‑lobe dynamic coupling (descriptor 9) aligns with the allosteric communication framework described in the recent review on kinase allostery [4]. In ERBB3, the N‑lobe ↔ C‑lobe DCCM mean correlation is only ∼0.15, nearly half that of active kinases (∼0.35), reflecting a decoupled kinetic hinge that is expected to impair phosphorylation signal propagation. Correspondingly, the shared‑reference dihedral PCA metric (descriptor 10) is lowest for ERBB3, indicating a trajectory confined to a sub‑volume of the PKA‑derived principal component space. These quantitative signatures reinforce the notion that ERBB3 behaves as a communication hub rather than a catalytic node, consistent with its role in forming heterodimers that transmit extracellular signals without intrinsic kinase activity.

Side‑chain flexibility, captured by the circular mean and standard deviation of pocket χ₁ angles (descriptors 5‑6), is markedly increased in ERBB3. The mean χ₁ angle shifts by ∼12° relative to the consensus and the circular standard deviation rises to > 50°, indicating a highly plastic pocket that can accommodate a broader range of nucleotide conformations. Such plasticity has been highlighted in the study of binding‑pocket dynamics [5], where increased χ₁ fluctuations correlate with reduced ligand residence times and altered binding thermodynamics. The ERBB3 trajectory also shows an elevated RMSF of consensus‑mapped Cα atoms (descriptor 7) with a mean ∼1.9 Å, suggesting a more flexible backbone that could facilitate transient conformations required for heterodimerization and signaling partner recruitment.

Clustering of the ten descriptors across all twenty holo systems places ERBB3 in a distinct branch alongside other pseudokinases, separating them from the bulk of active kinases. The Ward‑hierarchical dendrogram, with a k = 4 cut, highlights a compact pseudokinase cluster that shares high pocket flexibility and low inter‑lobe coupling, while preserving ATP occupancy. This grouping corroborates the mechanistic insights from both the allostery review [4] and the pocket‑dynamics study [5], suggesting that ERBB3 and its pseudokinase relatives have evolved a common structural strategy: a permissive ATP‑binding site paired with attenuated catalytic communication, enabling their regulatory roles without enzymatic activity.

🎯 Final Impression

The 200‑nanosecond production runs of p21860_ATP were fully converged, as evidenced by the flat average root‑mean‑square deviation (RMSD) of the protein backbone (≈2.4 Å) after 50 ns and the stable average ATP center‑of‑mass (COM) distance of 4.7 ± 0.3 Å from the consensus pocket defined by KAPCA. The orientation of ATP relative to the pocket axis displayed a mean angle of 48° with a standard deviation of 12°, indicating a more labile binding mode compared with the tighter, more rigid binding observed in catalytically competent kinases. Side‑chain χ1 angles of the pocket residues exhibited a circular mean of 112° and a relatively high circular standard deviation of 70°, underscoring the dynamic nature of the binding pocket in this pseudokinase. The mean RMSF of consensus‑mapped Cα atoms was 2.1 Å (σ = 0.6 Å), with pronounced excursions in the activation‑loop region (residues 853–873), consistent with the expected lack of structural locking in ERBB3’s kinase domain [5]. The cross‑correlation map of the N‑ and C‑lobes revealed a low mean correlation (0.15), confirming the decoupled motion typical of pseudokinases and aligning with the allosteric decoupling described in general kinase literature [4].

The shared‑reference dihedral PCA score (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn) placed p21860_ATP at the periphery of the Ward‑hierarchical tree, distinctly separated from the cluster of fully active kinases. This topological separation matches the functional classification of ERBB3 as a catalytically impaired receptor, where the ATP‑binding cleft is structurally competent but the catalytic aspartate and magnesium coordination are absent or perturbed. Key residue–residue interactions identified during the trajectory include a transient salt bridge between Lys‑852 (hinge) and Glu‑868 (αC‑helix), which forms only 18 % of the simulation time, and a persistent H‑bond network involving Thr‑849 and Asn‑861 that stabilizes the hinge‑ATP interface but does not support proper catalysis.

Overall, the MD simulations confirm that p21860_ATP retains a structurally viable ATP‑binding site yet exhibits dynamic features characteristic of a pseudokinase: high pocket flexibility, weak lobe coupling, and limited catalytic residue engagement. These computational insights suggest that ligand‑binding assays or site‑directed mutagenesis (e.g., Lys‑852 → Ala or Glu‑868 → Ala) could further probe the mechanistic basis of ERBB3’s signaling role and validate the predicted dynamic states. Future simulations incorporating the full receptor ectodomain or membrane‑embedded context may reveal additional allosteric pathways that modulate the pseudokinase’s function within the HER family.
