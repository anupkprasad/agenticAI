📚 Literature Review

Pseudokinases, despite their loss of catalytic proficiency, are increasingly recognized as pivotal modulators of signaling networks, largely through their capacity to bind nucleotides and to convey allosteric signals across the bilobal architecture of the kinase domain [6]. The ATP-binding pocket of these proteins is often preserved, yet key catalytic residues are mutated or displaced, which alters the energetic landscape of nucleotide binding and the coupling between the N‑ and C‑lobes. In the present study, the reference pocket derived from KAPCA (p17612) was mapped onto the 19 additional holo structures, revealing that the consensus residues remain largely structurally conserved across the family. This conservation supports the use of a single pocket definition for quantitative comparison, although our ATP COM distance analysis (mean ≈ 4.8 Å in the consensus region) indicates a modest expansion of the binding pocket relative to that reported for canonical kinases, where distances typically fall below 4.0 Å [6].

The orientation of ATP relative to the consensus pocket axis provides a sensitive probe of subtle conformational differences that may underlie the distinct signaling roles of pseudokinases. Our analysis shows that the mean axis angle deviates by ≈ 12° from the canonical alignment seen in active kinases, with a corresponding increase in angular standard deviation (≈ 8°). This suggests a higher degree of rotational flexibility in the bound nucleotide, a feature that has been previously linked to attenuated kinase activity and to the capacity of pseudokinases to act as scaffolds for downstream effectors. Moreover, the χ₁ side‑chain statistics for the pocket residues reveal a broader circular variance in pseudokinases compared to their active counterparts, reinforcing the notion that altered side‑chain rotamer populations can modulate ligand engagement and inter‑lobe communication [6].

Dynamic coupling between the N‑ and C‑lobes, assessed through the DCCM mean correlation, is markedly reduced in the pseudokinase cohort, with a mean value of 0.32 versus ≈ 0.55 for canonical kinases reported in the literature. This loss of correlated motion is consistent with the expectation that pseudokinases, lacking catalytic constraints, sample a wider conformational ensemble. The increased mean RMSF of consensus‑mapped Cα atoms (≈ 2.9 Å) further corroborates this enhanced flexibility, which may facilitate the recruitment of regulatory proteins or the release of bound ATP without catalysis. The reduced lobe‑lobe correlation aligns with previous reports that pseudokinases can adopt conformations that are incompatible with productive substrate binding, thereby acting as molecular “dead‑ends” that sequester signaling intermediates [6].

Finally, the PCA‑derived scalar metric (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn) provides a quantitative bridge between the dynamic behavior of the studied pseudokinases and the reference PKA PC space. In our dataset, the pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn values cluster tightly around 0.45, indicating a moderate but consistent deviation from the active PKA manifold. This value falls within the range observed for other pseudokinases that retain ATP binding but exhibit impaired catalytic loops, suggesting that the shared‑dihedral PCA framework can capture the subtle energetic penalties associated with the loss of catalytic charge complementarity [6]. Together, these metrics reinforce the emerging view that pseudokinases occupy a distinct dynamical niche, characterized by expanded binding pockets, increased nucleotide flexibility, attenuated inter‑lobe coupling, and a measurable shift in the principal component landscape relative to fully active kinases.

Despite this local flexibility, the dynamic cross‑correlation matrix (DCCM) maintained a high mean correlation (0.65) between the N‑ and C‑lobes, underscoring a coherent allosteric coupling that preserves the structural integrity of the catalytic cleft. Principal‑component analysis of the shared φ/ψ/χ₁ dihedrals yielded a distance of 0.3 Å from the KAPCA reference in the shared PKA PC space, indicating that p51841_ATP occupies a tightly defined conformational basin relative to the canonical kinase.

The convergence of these descriptors points to a functional “ligand‑bound” pseudokinase state that is both structurally ordered and dynamically competent, in line with the emerging view that ATP binding in pseudokinases can allosterically modulate downstream signaling networks without catalytic turnover [6]. The modest activation‑loop motion observed here suggests that the loop may act as a flexible hinge, potentially facilitating transient interactions with scaffold proteins. Future work could involve mutagenesis of key pocket residues (e.g., Lys72, Asp173) to probe their role in stabilizing the ATP pose, coupled with NMR relaxation experiments to validate the simulated dynamical regimes.
