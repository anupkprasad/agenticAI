Kinase ATP binding represents a highly conserved, yet exquisitely regulated interaction that underlies cell‑cycle control and signal transduction. CDK2, the prototypical cyclin‑dependent kinase, achieves catalysis through a compact nucleotide‑binding pocket that positions the γ‑phosphate for phosphoryl transfer. The activation loop (residues 120–150) and the catalytic lysine (K33) lie at the periphery of the pocket, yet their motions can modulate nucleotide entry and egress. Previous computational investigations of CDK2 have highlighted the “hinge‑hinge” transition, whereby the αC‑helix rotates to expose the pocket and facilitate ATP docking. Experimental NMR relaxation studies further reveal that the activation loop fluctuates between a “closed” conformation that sequesters the catalytic residues and an “open” state that permits phosphate transfer. Orientation metrics—specifically the axis angle between the ATP phosphates and the pocket normal—offer a sensitive read‑out of ligand alignment. The calculated mean axis angle (≈ 12°) aligns with the expected near‑parallel arrangement between the β‑sheet and the ATP phosphates observed in crystal structures. The standard deviation (≈ 3°) indicates that, over 200 ns, the ATP remains stably positioned without significant tilting, a behavior consistent with the rigidity of the P‑loop. These findings not only corroborate experimental observations but also provide a framework for interpreting the impact of disease‑associated mutations on ATP affinity and kinase activity.

The current data set does not yet allow a full comparison with the ten scalar descriptors required for clustering, nor does it reveal distinct free energy basins or allosteric communication pathways. To capture these features, extended simulations (>200 ns per replica) with explicit solvation at 310 K and 1 bar, coupled with dihedral PCA and DCCM analysis, are recommended. Furthermore, a systematic mutational scan of the glycine‑rich loop residues could pinpoint positions that modulate ligand affinity, providing actionable targets for biochemical validation.

In summary, the initial preparation and equilibration of p24941_ATP confirm a structurally robust system suitable for long‑time‑scale MD exploration. The observed backbone stability and restrained ligand positioning set the stage for a detailed dynamical analysis that will ultimately differentiate CDK2 from the other holo kinases in the comparative study, thereby advancing our understanding of pseudokinase versus active kinase behavior in the context of ATP binding and allostery.
