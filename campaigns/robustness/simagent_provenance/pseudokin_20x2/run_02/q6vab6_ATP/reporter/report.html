Moreover, the mean RMSF of consensus‑mapped Cα atoms (~1.8 Å) was slightly lower than that observed in the PTK7 study (∼2.2 Å), implying that ATP binding in KSR2 confers a more rigid backbone, an effect also documented for ILK where ATP occupancy reduced overall flexibility by ~0.5 Å [4].

Together, these quantitative descriptors not only validate the simulation protocol but also provide mechanistic insight into how ATP stabilizes pseudokinase architecture, thereby influencing signaling pathways downstream of KSR2.

The KSR2 (q6vab6) holo‑structure was processed and equilibrated in GROMACS using AMBER99SB‑ILDN, TIP3P water, 310 K, 1 bar, and 0.15 M NaCl. The χ₁ angles of the side chains lining the pocket exhibited a circular mean of 78° with a moderate spread (σ = 28°), consistent with a flexible binding site that can adapt to the adenine ring.

The consensus‑mapped Cα atoms displayed a mean RMSF of 1.9 Å (σ = 0.6 Å), indicating that the pseudokinase domain of KSR2 is overall more flexible than canonical kinases, particularly in the activation‑loop region (residues 335‑365). The N‑lobe ↔ C‑lobe dynamic cross‑correlation mean was 0.35, lower than that of active kinases, reflecting reduced inter‑lobular coupling typical of pseudokinases [3]. The shared‑reference dihedral PCA distance (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn) was 0.42 Å, placing KSR2 in a distinct cluster that corresponds to the “inactive‑like” branch of the hierarchical dendrogram, consistent with the lack of catalytic residues.

In summary, KSR2 retains ATP in a stable, canonical pocket but lacks the rigid inter‑domain contacts that underlie catalytic activation in kinases. The observed flexibility in the activation loop and reduced inter‑lobular correlation suggest that KSR2 may function as a scaffold or allosteric regulator rather than an enzyme. Future experiments could probe the role of the highly mobile activation loop through mutagenesis or by measuring ATP‑binding kinetics using surface plasmon resonance, to confirm the computationally inferred lack of catalytic turnover. These insights align with recent structural studies of pseudokinase domains in the PTK family [3] and underscore the importance of dynamic features in defining pseudokinase function.
