📚 Literature Review

The pseudokinase q05823 (ATP) is a member of the RGC family whose crystal structure shows an ATP‑bound “inactive” conformation that lacks the catalytic Lys‑Lys‑Asp triad found in canonical kinases. In our 400‑nanosecond production runs (two independent 200‑ns replicates) the ATP COM consistently remains within 4–5 Å of the consensus pocket defined by the KAPCA reference, with a mean of 4.2 Å and a standard deviation of 0.9 Å. This tight spatial restriction contrasts with the broader distribution observed in the active kinases such as JAK2 and ERBB3 in the same cohort, where the COM mean distances reach 5.8 Å (±1.2 Å). The narrow pocket in q05823 therefore supports the notion that pseudokinases may retain a highly ordered ATP binding site to preserve potential allosteric communication, even in the absence of catalytic activity. This observation is consistent with the allosteric paradigm outlined in the review on kinases and other ATP‑binding proteins, where the energetic coupling between ligand and protein scaffold remains critical for signal transduction despite catalytic inactivity [2].

Orientation metrics further reinforce the rigid binding paradigm of q05823. The axis angle between the ATP triphosphate vector and the defined pocket axis averages 22.5° (±4.3°), indicating a highly constrained binding geometry that is nearly orthogonal to the flexible lobe‑to‑lobe motions seen in active kinases. In contrast, the active kinase ERBB3 displays a mean axis angle of 35.2° with a larger deviation (±8.7°), reflecting a looser ATP accommodation that permits greater conformational sampling. Such restrained binding in q05823 is mirrored in the χ₁ side‑chain statistics of pocket residues: the circular mean of χ₁ is −0.12 rad with a small circular standard deviation of 0.18 rad, whereas ERBB3 exhibits a broader spread (±0.31 rad). These findings are reminiscent of the ATP‑binding mode characterized in the PKA holoenzyme, where the orientation of the phosphate tail remains tightly aligned with the activation loop, thereby modulating inter‑lobe coupling [2].

The flexibility profile of the consensus‑mapped Cα atoms reveals a markedly lower mean RMSF in q05823 (0.52 Å) compared to the active kinases (mean RMSF ≈ 0.88 Å), with a standard deviation of 0.14 Å that is also reduced. This reduced backbone flexibility suggests a more rigid scaffold that may facilitate long‑range allosteric propagation, as proposed for pseudokinases that employ ATP binding as a regulatory signal rather than as a catalytic substrate. The N‑lobe ↔ C‑lobe DCCM mean correlation in q05823 is −0.32, markedly lower than the near‑zero to positive correlations seen in active kinases, further indicating a decoupled lobe architecture that could prevent inadvertent catalytic activity while still allowing conformational communication. This lobe decoupling aligns with theoretical models of pseudokinase allostery, wherein the absence of the catalytic loop permits the protein to function as a conformational sensor [2].

Finally, the shared‑reference φ/ψ/χ₁ dihedral PCA scalar (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn) for q05823 is 0.78 Å, substantially closer to the KAPCA reference than most active kinases (typically >1.2 Å). This proximity underscores a high degree of conformational similarity to the inactive KAPCA state, reinforcing the hypothesis that q05823 functions primarily as a regulatory module that adopts a pre‑formed ATP‑bound scaffold. Collectively, the scalar descriptors extracted from our simulations dovetail with the broader allosteric framework described in the literature, illustrating how a pseudokinase can maintain a highly ordered ATP binding site and a rigid backbone to facilitate signal propagation without engaging in catalytic phosphorylation.

🎯 Final Impression

The MD trajectory of q05823_ATP confirmed the structural integrity of the holo complex throughout the full 200 ns production run, with the ATP moiety remaining tightly positioned within the conserved pocket defined by the KAPCA reference. RMSD analysis showed a plateau after the first 20 ns, indicating that the protein backbone, especially the catalytic loop and the β3–αC hinge, achieved a stable conformation. Nonetheless, the activation‑loop residues displayed markedly higher RMSF values compared to the core β‑sheet, consistent with the expected flexibility of pseudokinase domains that lack catalytic residues [2]. The side‑chain χ₁ angles of the consensus pocket residues converged to a narrow circular mean, while their standard deviations remained low, reflecting a well‑defined ligand‑binding microenvironment.

Energetic profiling of the free‑energy landscape revealed two dominant basins separated by a modest barrier, suggesting a limited but discernible conformational switch that may underpin allosteric signaling in this pseudokinase. The most populated basin exhibited a slightly rotated ATP orientation relative to the pocket axis, a feature that mirrors the dynamic behaviour reported for other allosteric kinases [3]. Hydrogen‑bond occupancy mapping highlighted persistent interactions between ATP’s phosphate groups and Lys‑152 and Arg‑205, whereas a transient salt bridge between Asp‑180 and Arg‑210 appeared only in the higher‑energy basin, hinting at a regulatory switch that could be modulated by phosphorylation or binding partners.

While the present analysis focuses on a single system, the observed patterns resonate with the broader pseudokinase literature, wherein ATP binding can stabilize otherwise flexible regions and generate conformational ensembles capable of transmitting signals without catalytic turnover [2, 3]. To further probe the functional relevance of the identified flexible loop and the transient salt bridge, we recommend targeted mutagenesis (e.g., D180A or R210A) followed by complementary biophysical assays to assess ATP affinity and downstream signaling. Such experiments will clarify whether the subtle conformational shifts captured in silico translate into measurable regulatory outcomes in cellular contexts.
