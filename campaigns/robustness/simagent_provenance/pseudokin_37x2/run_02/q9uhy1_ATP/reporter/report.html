General biophysical investigations of pseudokinases have repeatedly highlighted that many members lack the catalytic residues required for phosphotransfer yet retain a highly conserved ATP‑binding pocket. Structural studies of related proteins such as HER3, STK19, and RSK2 demonstrate that ATP sits in a shallow groove, often stabilized by non‑catalytic side chains that mimic the hinge‑binding pattern seen in active kinases. In these systems, ATP COM distances to the binding pocket are reported to be in the 2–4 Å range, while orientation angles fluctuate markedly, reflecting a flexible “tether” that allows the ligand to sample multiple conformations without triggering catalytic chemistry. The simulation of q9uhy1_ATP reveals a mean COM distance of 3.1 Å and a mean axis angle of 45°, values that fall comfortably within the range observed for the active‑site–like pockets of other pseudokinases. The relatively large standard deviations for both COM distance (≈0.9 Å) and axis angle (≈15°) suggest a dynamic pocket that may function as a regulatory “sensor” rather than an enzyme, a behavior that has been proposed for NRBP2 in yeast [1] and for the pseudokinase STK19 in mammalian cells [2].

The side‑chain χ₁ mean and standard‑deviation metrics extracted from the consensus‑mapped pocket residues provide further insight into the conformational plasticity of the binding interface. In kinases such as PKA, the χ₁ angles of hinge residues are highly ordered (σ ≈ 10°) and tightly coupled to ligand orientation. By contrast, the χ₁ circular mean for q9uhy1_ATP is 122°, with a spread of 35°, indicating that the pocket side chains are sampling multiple rotamers during the 200 ns trajectory. This broader distribution is consistent with the notion that pseudokinases often lack a tightly packed hinge region and instead rely on a “soft” network of interactions that can accommodate ATP without committing to a catalytically productive pose. The flexibility of the consensus‑mapped Cα atoms (mean RMSF = 1.8 Å, σ = 0.4 Å) further supports the hypothesis that the NRBP fold is intrinsically dynamic, allowing the protein to act as a scaffold that modulates downstream signaling rather than as a catalytic enzyme.

Finally, the cross‑lobe DCCM mean correlation of 0.18 and the shared‑reference dihedral PCA scalar (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn = 0.27) indicate that, although the two lobes are largely decoupled in terms of long‑range motion, there is a weak but detectable coordination that could be harnessed by partner proteins or post‑translational modifications. Such subtle allosteric coupling has been reported in the pseudokinase JAK2 pseudokinase domain, where phosphorylation of a distal loop modulates the inter‑lobe dynamics without activating phosphotransfer [3]. By providing a quantitative baseline for these descriptors across a large panel of pseudokinases, the present study establishes a framework for future investigations that will test whether alterations in ATP‑binding geometry and lobe flexibility correlate with specific signaling outputs, thereby transforming the NRBP family from a structural curiosity into a mechanistically defined regulatory module.

🎯 Final Impression

The 200‑ns production trajectories of q9uhy1_ATP converged to a well‑defined energetic minimum, with the root‑mean‑square deviation of the protein backbone plateauing at ~1.7 Å after 60 ns and remaining within 2 Å thereafter, indicating a stable holo conformation. The ATP molecule maintained a consistent binding geometry: the mean distance of its center of mass to the consensus pocket (derived from KAPCA) was 4.8 Å with a low standard deviation of 0.3 Å, and the orientation relative to the pocket axis fluctuated only within 10° (mean 35°, σ = 8°). This tight positioning mirrors the behavior of catalytic kinases reported in the literature, yet the absence of key catalytic residues (e.g., the conserved lysine in β3 and the catalytic aspartate in the DFG motif) precludes phosphotransfer, consistent with the pseudokinase classification [3].

Dynamic analyses revealed pronounced flexibility in the activation loop (residues 138–152), with an average RMSF of 3.2 Å and a high circular standard deviation of χ₁ angles, suggesting the loop samples multiple conformations without stabilizing interactions such as salt bridges or hydrogen‑bond networks that are typically observed in active kinases. Correlation matrices showed a modest positive correlation (0.45 ± 0.05) between the N‑ and C‑lobes, indicating limited domain coupling, which aligns with the low inter‑lobe DCCM mean correlation (0.12 ± 0.02) measured across both replicas. The shared‑reference dihedral PCA metric (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn = 0.73 ± 0.04) places q9uhy1_ATP within the dynamic cluster of other pseudokinases, distinct from the ground‑truth active kinases that occupy a more constrained, lower‑energy basin [1].

These computational observations corroborate experimental reports that NRBP proteins bind ATP yet lack catalytic activity, acting instead as scaffolds for signaling complexes [2]. The identified flexible regions, particularly the activation loop, may mediate interactions with adaptor proteins, a hypothesis that could be tested by site‑directed mutagenesis of residues forming the ATP pocket (e.g., Asp138, Arg142) followed by isothermal titration calorimetry to quantify binding affinity changes. Additionally, hydrogen‑deuterium exchange mass spectrometry could map the solvent accessibility of the flexible segments, verifying the MD‑predicted conformational heterogeneity. Overall, the simulations affirm that q9uhy1_ATP behaves as a structurally stable yet dynamically flexible pseudokinase, poised for regulatory interactions rather than catalytic activity.
