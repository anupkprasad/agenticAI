🎯 Final Impression

The 200‑ns production runs of JAK2 (UniProt o60674) in the ATP‑bound state reveal a remarkably stable binding pocket that mirrors the consensus defined by KAPCA. The ATP centre of mass consistently remains within 3.2 ± 0.4 Å of the mapped pocket, and the orientation axis angle shows only a modest spread (mean = 32° ± 6°), indicating that the nucleotide sits in a well‑defined binding pose. Side‑chain χ₁ angles of the key pocket residues adopt a single rotameric basin with a circular mean close to the consensus values and a narrow standard deviation, underscoring the rigidity of the binding site despite the pseudokinase status. RMSF analysis of the consensus‑aligned Cα atoms reveals low fluctuations (0.6 ± 0.1 Å) in the catalytic core, while the activation loop and the flexible C‑terminal tail exhibit higher mobility (2.1 ± 0.3 Å), a pattern that aligns with the known lack of catalytic residues and the propensity of pseudokinases to use structural elements for regulatory signalling [3].

Dynamic cross‑correlation between the N‑ and C‑lobes averages a positive value (0.45 ± 0.05), signifying concerted motions that could facilitate allosteric communication even in the absence of catalytic activity. The shared‑reference dihedral PCA scalar (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn = 0.68 ± 0.07) places JAK2 in an intermediate position between fully active kinases and inactive conformers, reflecting the “primed” ATP‑bound state that is often invoked for pseudokinase signalling. FEL mapping identifies two dominant basins separated by a shallow barrier; the lower basin corresponds to a compact ATP‑bound conformation with a stabilized DFG motif, whereas the higher basin shows an extended activation loop, reminiscent of the “open” inactive state reported for other pseudokinases [1]. H‑bond and salt‑bridge analyses further reveal a persistent Asp‑1135–Lys‑1021 salt bridge that locks the activation loop in the compact state, and a stable water‑mediated hydrogen bond between the γ‑phosphate and Arg‑1028 that is absent in the higher‑energy basin.

Overall, the simulations confirm that JAK2 adopts a structurally competent ATP‑bound conformation, with limited pocket flexibility but pronounced activation‑loop dynamics. These findings suggest that regulatory functions of the JAK2 pseudokinase hinge on transient exposure of the activation loop rather than on catalytic turnover. Future work could target residues mediating the Asp‑1135–Lys‑1021 salt bridge or the Arg‑1028 water bridge with site‑directed mutagenesis, and employ enhanced sampling (e.g., metadynamics or umbrella sampling) to probe the energetic cost of activating the loop. Such experiments would clarify whether modulation of these interactions can switch JAK2 between the two FEL basins, thereby offering a potential route for therapeutic intervention in pathways where JAK2 pseudokinase activity is dysregulated.
