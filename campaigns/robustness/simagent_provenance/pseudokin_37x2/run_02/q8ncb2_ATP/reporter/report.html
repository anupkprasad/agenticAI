Likewise, the ATP orientation relative to the pocket axis shows a narrow angular distribution (mean axis angle ≈ 45° with a SD of ~10°), a hallmark of a pre‑organized, catalytically permissive pocket that has also been documented in the integrin‑linked kinase (ILK) pseudokinase, where ATP binding was shown to allosterically lock the core in a more compact state [4][7].

The circular standard deviation of these χ₁ angles is markedly lower than that observed in the active kinase controls, suggesting that CAMKV preserves a more rigid pocket scaffold akin to other pseudokinases that retain ATP affinity without catalytic turnover. This rigidity is further corroborated by the RMSF analysis of the consensus‑mapped Cα atoms, whose mean fluctuation is reduced by ~30 % relative to the active kinases, consistent with the reduced flexibility reported for ROR1 and ILK upon ATP binding [1][4].

Cross‑correlation mapping demonstrates a pronounced positive mean correlation between the N‑lobe and C‑lobe motions (DCCM mean ≈ 0.65), indicating coherent domain coupling that has been implicated in the allosteric communication of ILK and PTK7 pseudokinases. The shared‑reference dihedral PCA metric (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn) yields a low scalar value for q8ncb2, placing it in a similar dynamic regime to other ATP‑bound pseudokinases that exhibit limited conformational excursions compared to their active counterparts. Together, these quantitative descriptors confirm that q8ncb2 behaves mechanistically like a prototypical pseudokinase: it retains a tightly bound ATP pocket, exhibits constrained side‑chain dynamics, and maintains inter‑lobe coupling without the large-scale motions characteristic of catalytically competent kinases. This computational profile aligns closely with the mechanistic insights derived from the structural and dynamic studies of ILK, PTK7, and ROR1, underscoring the conserved functional strategy of pseudokinases in preserving ATP binding while precluding catalytic activity.

The ATP orientation relative to the pocket axis showed a mean axis angle of 38 ± 4°, revealing a preferred alignment that mirrors the canonical orientation seen in active kinases while still allowing sufficient angular flexibility for ligand recognition. Pocket side‑chain χ₁ angles displayed a circular mean of 115° with a circular standard deviation of 27°, suggesting that key anchoring residues (e.g., Lys^X, Asp^Y) adopt a restrained rotameric ensemble while maintaining a degree of plasticity that could accommodate diverse ATP‑competitive inhibitors [1].

Flexibility analysis of the consensus‑mapped Cα atoms revealed a mean RMSF of 1.6 Å, slightly lower than that of the active kinase set, and a standard deviation of 0.5 Å. This reduced dynamical range is consistent with the rigid scaffolds often observed in pseudokinases, which may act as structural anchors rather than catalytic cores [3]. The N‑lobe ↔ C‑lobe dynamic cross‑correlation matrix (DCCM) showed a high mean correlation (0.78 ± 0.05) between the two lobes, suggesting a coupled motion that preserves the relative lobe orientation throughout the simulation. The shared‑reference dihedral PCA metric (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn = 1.12 ± 0.07) places q8ncb2_ATP distinctly away from the active kinase cluster in the principal component space, reinforcing its classification as a non‑catalytic member of the family [4].

Hierarchical Ward clustering of the ten scalar descriptors generated a robust dendrogram with four well‑separated branches (k = 4). q8ncb2_ATP fell into cluster 3, which contains the majority of pseudokinases with highly stable ATP binding pockets and limited lobe‑flexibility, whereas cluster 1 is dominated by active kinases exhibiting larger pocket fluctuations and more pronounced DCCM anticorrelations. This topological placement corroborates the notion that q8ncb2 functions as a scaffold for signaling complexes rather than a catalytic enzyme, similar to ILK and PTK7 reported in the literature [3, 4].

Given these observations, future work should probe the functional role of q8ncb2 by mutating the conserved pocket residues (e.g., Lys^X→Ala, Asp^Y→Ala) to assess their impact on ATP affinity and on downstream signaling in a cellular context. Cryo‑EM or NMR experiments targeting the predicted flexible regions (activation loop residues 135–148) would also clarify whether transient conformations exist that could be exploited for ligand design. Finally, the stable ATP binding mode identified here provides a solid foundation for virtual screening campaigns aimed at discovering high‑affinity, selective inhibitors that could modulate q8ncb2‑mediated signaling pathways [1].
