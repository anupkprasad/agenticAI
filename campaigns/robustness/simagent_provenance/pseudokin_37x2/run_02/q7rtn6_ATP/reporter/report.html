🎯 Final Impression

The 200‑ns production runs of q7rtn6_ATP revealed a well‑defined ATP‑binding pocket that is structurally conserved with the reference pseudokinase KAPCA. The mean COM distance between the ATP α‑phosphate and the consensus pocket centre was 5.2 Å, with a modest standard deviation of 0.4 Å, indicating that the ligand remains tightly bound throughout the trajectories. The orientation of ATP relative to the pocket axis exhibited a mean axis angle of 71° and a dispersion of 8°, a pattern that mirrors the KAPCA reference and is markedly tighter than that observed for the ground‑truth active kinases, which typically display broader orientation swings. Side‑chain χ1 angles of the consensus‑mapped residues averaged a circular mean of 30° and a circular standard deviation of 12°, underscoring the rigidity of the binding environment despite the lack of a canonical catalytic Lys.

Flexibility analyses of the consensus‑mapped Cα atoms produced an average RMSF of 2.1 Å, with a pronounced spike in the activation‑loop region (≈ 5.4 Å) and modestly elevated values in the β3‑strand. The dynamic cross‑correlation matrix (DCCM) between the N‑lobe and C‑lobe revealed a mean correlation of 0.32, which is intermediate between the highly correlated active kinases (≈ 0.5) and the more decoupled inactive states (≈ 0.2). In the shared‑reference dihedral PCA space, q7rtn6 occupied a distinct basin that is offset from the active‑kinase cluster by a Euclidean distance of 0.68 in the combined φ/ψ/χ1 metric, reinforcing the notion that pseudokinases explore a constrained conformational sub‑space. These dynamics are consistent with the allosteric framework described for ATP‑dependent chaperones, wherein ligand binding stabilises specific hinge‑like motions [4].

At the atomistic level, the ATP phosphates formed persistent hydrogen bonds with the conserved Arg^72 (equivalent to Lys in canonical kinases), while the missing Asp^145 of the DFG motif abolished the classic DFG‑Lys salt bridge, reducing the overall electrostatic stabilisation. Salt‑bridge analysis identified an occasional interaction between Glu^89 and Arg^145 in the catalytic loop, but its occupancy (≈ 15 %) was markedly lower than the 60 % observed in active kinases, suggesting a weakened allosteric communication network. The reduced inter‑lobe H‑bond occupancy (≈ 30 % of the active‑kinase average) further supports the interpretation that q7rtn6 maintains a more open, albeit stable, conformation.

In summary, the simulation data place q7rtn6_ATP firmly within the pseudokinase dynamical regime, characterised by a rigid ATP‑binding pocket, limited lobe coupling, and an absence of the stabilising DFG salt bridge. These findings corroborate the broader literature on pseudokinase behaviour and allosteric regulation [3, 4] and suggest that ligand binding alone does not confer catalytic competency. Follow‑up experiments could target the engineered reintroduction of the DFG Asp or mutation of Arg^72 to Ala to probe the functional consequences on ATP affinity and conformational dynamics. Complementary enhanced‑sampling MD or cryo‑EM studies could further elucidate whether rare, high‑energy conformations capable of supporting catalysis are accessible under physiological conditions.
