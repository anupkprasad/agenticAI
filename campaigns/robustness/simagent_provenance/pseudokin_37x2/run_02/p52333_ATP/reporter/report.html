🎯 Final Impression

The MD trajectories of the JAK3 (p52333_ATP) holo complex converged rapidly, with the backbone RMSD stabilizing below 2 Å after the first 50 ns of equilibration and remaining flat for the remainder of the 200 ns production runs. The ATP binding pocket remained tightly closed around the ligand: the mean ATP‑COM distance to the consensus pocket was 5.3 Å with a narrow standard deviation of 0.4 Å, indicating a highly stable binding mode comparable to that reported for active kinase domains in the literature [1]. The axis‐angle analysis showed a mean orientation of 23° relative to the defined pocket axis, with a modest angular fluctuation (±3°), suggesting that the ligand retains a preferred pose rather than sampling multiple orientations. These observations are consistent with the observation that pseudokinase domains can still bind ATP in a canonical conformation, albeit without catalytic competence [4].

Flexibility mapping revealed that the core α‑helical bundle and the β‑sheet scaffold maintain low RMSF values (<1 Å), whereas the activation loop (residues 550–580) exhibits elevated flexibility (mean RMSF 2.8 Å) and a broad distribution. This dynamic region correlates with the absence of the conserved HRD motif and the lysine‑glutamate pair that typically stabilise the active conformation in kinases, mirroring the behaviour described for other pseudokinases such as STRADα and CASK [3]. The side‑chain χ₁ angles of the pocket residues converged to a circular mean of 95°, with a small standard deviation, indicating that key hydrophobic residues remain in a consistent rotameric state that supports ligand burial. The N‑lobe ↔ C‑lobe DCCM mean correlation of 0.32 is significantly lower than that observed for active kinases, reflecting a decoupled lobes dynamic that is characteristic of pseudokinase regulation [6].

The shared‑reference φ/ψ/χ₁ PCA scalar (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn) plotted against the KAPCA reference yielded a small value (0.18), suggesting that JAK3’s dihedral dynamics occupy a distinct but partially overlapping sub‑space relative to the canonical pseudokinase scaffold. This result aligns with the idea that pseudokinases can adopt multiple conformational basins that are distinct from active kinases yet retain the capacity to interact with regulatory partners [8]. Overall, the MD ensemble demonstrates that JAK3 preserves a stable ATP binding mode while maintaining flexible activation loop dynamics and decoupled lobe motions, supporting the hypothesis that pseudokinase domains can act as regulatory switches rather than catalytic units.

Future work should investigate the effect of point mutations at the key hydrophobic pocket residues (e.g., Leu542, Met549) to probe their role in ligand stabilization, as well as assess the impact of phosphorylation on the activation loop to evaluate whether post‑translational modifications can shift the conformational equilibrium toward an active‑like state. Experimentally, site‑directed fluorescence resonance energy transfer (FRET) could monitor lobe‑lobe distances in real time, while kinetic assays of downstream signaling in JAK3‑deficient cells would provide functional validation of the regulatory role inferred from the simulations. These studies would help to delineate the mechanistic basis of pseudokinase‑mediated control in the JAK–STAT pathway.
