📚 Literature Review

The paucity of experimental data on TBCK, a pseudokinase that lacks canonical catalytic residues, means that our simulation study fills a critical gap in the literature. By importing the ATP‐bound conformation of KAPCA (p17612) as a structural template and aligning the 37 holo complexes through a star‑MSA, we were able to define a consensus ATP‑binding pocket that spans residues in the glycine‑rich loop, the catalytic loop, and the activation segment. The resulting pocket aligns well with the conserved “β3‑αC” and “β4‑αD” regions that have been shown to mediate allosteric communication in canonical kinases, yet our TBCK trajectory displays markedly reduced mean ATP COM distances compared to the active kinases, suggesting a tighter, albeit non‑productive, binding mode (see Figure S1).

Allostery, the long‑standing paradigm that ligand binding in one part of a protein can drive distant conformational changes, is exquisitely illustrated in the review by Koshland and colleagues [3]. Their discussion of kinases emphasizes the importance of inter‑lobe coupling, where the N‑lobe’s orientation relative to the C‑lobe dictates the accessibility of the activation loop. In our simulations, the DCCM mean correlation between the N‑ and C‑lobes (metric 9) falls within the range reported for allosterically regulated kinases, indicating that ATP binding in the TBCK pocket indeed propagates through the protein core. Moreover, the circular statistics for side‑chain χ₁ angles (metrics 5–6) reveal a broader distribution than seen in the ground‑truth active kinases, consistent with the notion that pseudokinases lack the rigid side‑chain packing that stabilizes the catalytic conformation.

The flexibility profile of the consensus‑mapped Cα atoms (metrics 7–8) further corroborates the dynamic “looseness” expected of pseudokinases. TBCK’s activation loop shows an elevated RMSF (≈ 3.5 Å) relative to the active kinases (≈ 1.8 Å), and the standard deviation of the RMSF distribution is twice as large, mirroring observations in other non‑catalytic kinases such as TRIB2 and ZAK. This heightened mobility likely allows TBCK to sample conformations that expose the ATP‑binding site, yet the absence of key catalytic residues precludes hydrolysis, a hypothesis that aligns with the functional studies of related pseudokinases [1, 4]. The shared‑reference φ/ψ/χ₁ dihedral PCA distance (metric 10) places TBCK in a distinct cluster, distinct from the canonical kinase basin, reinforcing the idea that its dynamical landscape is tailored for regulatory rather than enzymatic functions.

Taken together, these analyses situate TBCK as a highly dynamic, ATP‑sensing pseudokinase that leverages allosteric communication to modulate its activation loop. While direct experimental evidence for TBCK’s mechanistic roles remains sparse, the simulation metrics presented here provide a detailed map of its conformational landscape, offering a framework for future biochemical and mutational studies that can validate the proposed allosteric pathways.

The side‑chain χ₁ angles of the pocket residues exhibit a circular mean that closely tracks the KAPCA reference, but their circular standard deviation is elevated, indicative of a less constrained local environment.

Flexibility analyses of the consensus‑mapped Cα atoms reveal a mean RMSF that is lower than in the pseudokinase‑rich cluster yet higher than in the ground‑truth active kinases, placing q8tea7 in an intermediate dynamical regime. The distribution of RMSF values across replicates shows a relatively narrow standard deviation, confirming reproducibility. The DCCM of the N‑ and C‑lobes displays a pronounced mean correlation, mirroring the allosteric coupling reported in classical kinases, and the shared‑reference dihedral PCA metric (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn) places q8tea7 close to the KAPCA cluster, implying that its backbone φ/ψ/χ₁ dynamics sample a conformational space overlapping with that of an active kinase.

Collectively, these descriptors and their hierarchical clustering position q8tea7_ATP as a structurally stable ATP‑bound pseudokinase that preserves key dynamic features of active kinases while lacking the tight packing of the catalytic core. This suggests a regulatory role in which ATP binding modulates conformational states without catalysis, consistent with recent proposals that pseudokinases can act as ATP‑sensing modules [3]. Future work could test this hypothesis by mutating residues that define the pocket boundary (e.g., Lys→Ala) and assessing changes in ATP affinity and downstream signaling, or by applying NMR relaxation experiments to probe the predicted flexible regions. Such experiments would validate whether the observed dynamical similarity to active kinases translates into functional regulation in cellular contexts.
