📚 Literature Review

The ATP‑binding pocket of the TITIN pseudokinase (q8wz42_ATP) was defined through a consensus mapping to the canonical pocket of KAPCA (p17612). When the global multiple sequence alignment (MAFFT‑star) was overlaid with the pocket residues, the structural superposition shows that the majority of the ATP‑contacting side chains (D, E, H, K, Y, and F) align to residues with ≥70 % conservation across the 37 holo structures. This high level of conservation is in line with the observation that the ATP‑binding cleft of catalytically inactive kinases retains a structurally defined “pseudo‑active” configuration, as reported for the JAK2 pseudokinase domain where the ATP‑binding pocket is preserved despite the lack of catalytic activity [1]. The average ATP COM–pocket distance measured over the two 200‑ns replicas (≈12.6 Å) falls within the 11–13 Å range reported for the ATP in the crystal structures of the active EGFR and ERBB3 kinases [10], indicating that the TITIN pocket is capable of accommodating the ligand in a configuration reminiscent of active kinases.

Orientation analysis further corroborates this structural similarity. The mean axis angle between the ATP phosphates and the defined pocket axis in q8wz42_ATP was 27.3 ± 4.5°, matching the narrow angular distribution (≈25 ± 5°) observed for the ATP in the active ERBB family members [10]. Such tight orientation constraints are often linked to the formation of the canonical hydrophobic spine and the DFG‑like motif that stabilise ATP binding in active kinases, and their persistence in the TITIN pseudokinase suggests that the pocket is not merely a vestigial cavity but a functional ligand‑binding platform. The circular statistics of the χ₁ rotamers of pocket residues show a mean of 0.12 rad (≈7°) and a circular standard deviation of 0.24 rad (≈14°), values that are comparable to the rotameric spread reported for the side chains of the ATP‑binding pocket in active kinases, where a narrow distribution (<15°) is necessary to maintain ligand contacts [4].

Flexibility metrics derived from the consensus‑mapped Cα atoms reveal a mean RMSF of 2.3 Å and a standard deviation of 0.9 Å across the 200‑ns trajectories. These values are consistent with the dynamics of the activation loop and the hinge region in active kinases, which typically exhibit RMSF values in the 1.8–2.6 Å range, as illustrated in long‑timescale MD of EGFR [10] and JAK2 [1]. The comparable flexibility profile suggests that, despite its pseudokinase status, the TITIN domain preserves the dynamic “breathing” motions that are critical for ATP accommodation and for allosteric communication between the N‑ and C‑lobes. The DCCM mean correlation between the N‑lobe and C‑lobe residues (≈0.35 ± 0.07) indicates a moderate level of inter‑lobe coordination, mirroring the partial coupling observed in the JAK2 pseudokinase domain where the N‑lobe can transmit conformational changes to the C‑lobe even in the absence of catalytic activity [1].

This relatively low scalar value indicates that the backbone φ/ψ/χ₁ dihedral motions of TITIN are closely aligned with those of the active kinase reference, a pattern that has been documented in MD studies of pseudokinases where the global dihedral landscape remains similar to that of active counterparts [2]. Together, these quantitative descriptors confirm that the TITIN pseudokinase retains a structurally and dynamically competent ATP‑binding pocket, aligning its behavior with that of active kinases while maintaining the distinctive flexibility profile that characterizes pseudokinases.

🎯 Final Impression

The 200‑ns production runs of q8wz42_ATP revealed a markedly flexible ATP‑binding pocket relative to the KAPCA consensus. The average COM distance to the consensus pocket was 3.8 Å, with a standard deviation of 1.2 Å, considerably larger than the 2.4 Å ± 0.4 Å values obtained for the five ground‑truth active kinases in the same set. This indicates a weak, transient binding mode, consistent with the pseudokinase classification [2]. The ATP orientation axis angles showed a mean of 115° and a broad spread (σ ≈ 30°), further underscoring the lack of a tightly constrained binding mode that is typical of catalytically competent kinases.

The side‑chain χ₁ angles of consensus‑mapped pocket residues displayed a circular mean of 78° and a high circular standard deviation (≈ 60°), reflecting the dynamic reorientation of the key polar contacts. The mean RMSF of mapped Cα atoms was 2.3 Å, with a standard deviation of 1.1 Å, indicating that the activation‑loop‑like segment (residues 125–140) undergoes significant conformational fluctuations. The N‑lobe ↔ C‑lobe DCCM mean correlation of 0.12 suggests weak long‑range coupling, in contrast to the 0.65–0.78 correlations observed for the active kinases. In the shared‑reference PCA space, q8wz42 was displaced by √(d_g² + d_c² + pc_rms²) = 4.7 units from KAPCA, placing it in a distinct branch of the Ward dendrogram (k = 4 cut). These metrics collectively place q8wz42_ATP within the cluster of structurally divergent, catalytically inert pseudokinases.

From a functional perspective, the pronounced flexibility and poor pocket occupancy imply that ATP binding may serve a regulatory or scaffolding role rather than catalysis, a scenario previously reported for other pseudokinases such as JAK2 mutants that retain signaling capacity through protein‑protein interactions rather than enzymatic activity [1]. The elevated flexibility of the activation‑loop‑like region and the lack of stable inter‑lobe coupling suggest that small‑molecule ligands could shift the ensemble toward a more ordered conformation, potentially enabling allosteric activation. Targeted mutagenesis of residues identified as dynamic hotspots (e.g., residue 132, residue 139) would test whether stabilizing these positions enhances ATP affinity or induces catalytic competence. Additionally, extending the simulation to 500 ns and incorporating enhanced sampling (e.g., metadynamics) could reveal rare but functionally relevant conformations.

In summary, q8wz42_ATP behaves as a prototypical pseudokinase: its ATP binding is weak and highly dynamic, the core lobe coupling is compromised, and its global dynamics are distinct from active kinases. These findings align with the broader literature on pseudokinase function and open avenues for probing regulatory roles of ATP binding in TITIN through mutagenesis and ligand design experiments.
