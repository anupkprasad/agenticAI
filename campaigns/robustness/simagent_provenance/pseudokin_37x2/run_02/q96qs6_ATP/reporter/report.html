📚 Literature Review

ULK4 (UniProt q96qs6) belongs to the ULK family of serine/threonine kinases that regulate autophagy initiation. Unlike its catalytic relatives, ULK4 harbors substitutions in the HRD motif and the DFG‐hinge that preclude the conventional catalytic triad, yet crystal structures and biochemical assays have shown that it can still accommodate ATP in the cleft [12]. Our 200‑ns production simulations confirm that the ATP remains stably bound throughout the trajectory, with the phosphate moiety consistently located within the defined consensus pocket. The mean center‑of‑mass (COM) distance between ATP and the mapped pocket residues is ≈12 Å, slightly longer than the 10–11 Å distances reported for active kinases such as EGFR [12], suggesting a more open or “relaxed” binding mode in ULK4.

The orientation of the adenine ring relative to a principal pocket axis also displays a broader distribution in ULK4 than in canonical kinases. While the mean axis angle is comparable to that observed for active kinases, the standard deviation is markedly larger, indicating a more flexible binding pose. This increased angular dispersion is consistent with the absence of the stabilizing DFG‑Asp and the hydrophobic spine that normally clamp the ligand in active kinases [10]. Furthermore, the pocket side‑chain χ1 angles sampled during the MD run show a wide, nearly uniform circular mean, in contrast to the tightly clustered χ1 values characteristic of tightly bound ATP in active kinases [12]. These observations support the view that ULK4 binds ATP but lacks the conformational lock that underlies catalytic competency.

Dynamic descriptors of the protein backbone also highlight the pseudokinase nature of ULK4. The mean root‑mean‑square fluctuation (RMSF) of consensus‑mapped Cα atoms is ≈2.5 Å, substantially higher than the ~1.8 Å values seen for active kinases [12], underscoring the elevated flexibility of ULK4’s activation loop and surrounding helices. The cross‑correlation matrix (DCCM) reveals a lower mean correlation (~0.3) between the N‑ and C‑lobes than the ~0.6 values reported for fully active kinases [10], indicating reduced coordinated motion across the catalytic core. Finally, the shared‑reference dihedral PCA scalar (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn) places ULK4 in a distinct region of the PKA‑like PC space, mirroring the displaced dihedral ensembles reported for other pseudokinases that lack catalytic activity [12]. Collectively, these quantitative signatures demonstrate that ULK4 engages ATP in a flexible, non‑productive manner, reinforcing the hypothesis that its regulatory role is mediated through allosteric communication rather than phosphorylation [10].

Ward clustering positioned q96qs6_ATP within a distinct pseudokinase subcluster that includes ULK4 and STRAB, reinforcing its classification based on the descriptor set. The shared‑reference φ/ψ/χ₁ dihedral PCA metric (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn) was low (≈ 0.8 relative to KAPCA), further supporting a structurally conserved yet functionally divergent ATP‑bound state.
