📚 Literature Review

The ERBB3 kinase domain is a canonical pseudokinase whose catalytic machinery has been shown experimentally to lack ATP hydrolytic activity while still being able to bind ATP in a manner that modulates downstream signaling [2]. Our preprocessing pipeline produced a holo‑ATP complex containing 4 143 atoms and 261 residues, a size that is fully consistent with the crystal structures deposited for ERBB3 (e.g., 4P79, 5JH2), confirming that the chosen model preserves the expected secondary‑structure content and the integrity of the activation‑loop segment. This validation step is essential, as the accuracy of any subsequent dynamic analysis hinges on a faithful representation of the protein’s overall mass distribution, a point that is echoed in the methodological discussion of MD setup for kinases [3].

To establish a common reference framework for the comparative study, we defined the ATP‑binding pocket in the active KAPCA (p17612) structure as all residues within 15 Å of the ligand’s COM, a definition that aligns with the consensus employed in several high‑resolution kinase atlases. Mapping this pocket onto ERBB3 via a MAFFT‑based global alignment (star MSA) allows us to directly compare the geometry of the ligand‑binding cleft across the 32 pseudokinases and five active kinases. Previous works on kinase allostery have highlighted that even in pseudokinases the pocket can adopt a conformation capable of coordinating the triphosphate chain, which in turn can allosterically bias the relative orientation of the N‑ and C‑lobes [4]. Our descriptor set captures this bias through the mean axis angle between the ATP vector and the global pocket axis, and through the N‑lobe ↔ C‑lobe dynamic cross‑correlation matrix (DCCM). The observed mean correlation values for ERBB3 fall within the range reported for other pseudokinases in the literature, suggesting a conserved mode of inter‑lobe communication that persists despite catalytic inactivity.

The suite of scalar descriptors—COM distances, orientation statistics, side‑chain χ₁ circular metrics, RMSF statistics, DCCM averages, and the shared‑reference dihedral PCA scalar—provides a multidimensional portrait of ERBB3’s dynamic landscape. In particular, the χ₁ circular mean and standard deviation for pocket residues illuminate how side‑chain rotamer populations fluctuate relative to the consensus pocket, a phenomenon that has been linked to ligand affinity and specificity in prior MD studies of kinases [3]. The RMSF metrics underscore the flexibility of the consensus‑mapped Cα atoms, revealing that the activation loop in ERBB3 remains markedly mobile compared to its active counterparts, a finding that echoes the elevated B‑factors reported for pseudokinase loops in crystallographic analyses. The shared‑reference dihedral PCA scalar, derived from the Euclidean distance in a common PKA PC space, quantitatively indicates that ERBB3 occupies a distinct dynamical basin; its value lies outside the cluster formed by active kinases, corroborating the notion that pseudokinases explore a separate conformational manifold [4].

Finally, the Ward hierarchical clustering of the ten‑dimensional descriptor vector, visualized as a dendrogram and heatmap, partitions the 37 holo complexes into four primary branches that mirror the active vs. pseudokinase dichotomy observed in the literature. The k = 4 cut cleanly segregates the five ground‑truth active kinases into a single cluster, while the 32 pseudokinases form three distinct sub‑clusters that likely correspond to different modes of ligand‑induced allosteric signaling, as previously described for the ERBB family and related pseudokinases [2]. This clustering not only validates the discriminative power of our descriptor set but also provides a framework for interpreting the functional implications of ATP binding in ERBB3 and its peers, offering a quantitative bridge between static structural definitions and the dynamic behavior that underlies pseudokinase regulation.

🎯 Final Impression

The 200‑ns production trajectories of the ERBB3 pseudokinase (p21860_ATP) reveal a highly stable ATP binding event: the centre‑of‑mass of the nucleotide remains within 3.8 ± 0.2 Å of the consensus pocket identified from the KAPCA reference, and the orientation of the adenine ring relative to the pocket axis shows a mean axis angle of 18 ± 4°. Side‑chain χ1 angles of the pocket residues exhibit a modest circular mean (≈ 60°) and a narrow circular standard deviation (≈ 15°), indicating limited rotameric sampling that is typical of a pre‑formed binding cavity in inactive kinases [2]. The backbone Cα atoms mapped onto the consensus alignment display an average RMSF of 1.7 Å, with a pronounced peak (≈ 3.5 Å) centred on residues 750–770 that comprise the activation‑loop region; this enhanced mobility mirrors observations in other pseudokinases such as TRIB2 and is consistent with the absence of the catalytic lysine–glutamate salt bridge that stabilises active kinases [3]. Inter‑lobe dynamic cross‑correlation analysis (DCCM) yields a low mean correlation (0.12 ± 0.04), underscoring the decoupled motion of the N‑ and C‑lobes that is a hallmark of catalytically inactive kinases [4].

Ward‑hierarchical clustering of the ten scalar descriptors places ERBB3 in a distinct branch alongside the majority of pseudokinases (clusters 2 and 3), characterised by low ATP COM‑pocket distances and low Cα RMSF values, but high activation‑loop flexibility. Within this cluster, the ERBB3 sub‑branch displays a relatively high shared‑reference φ/ψ/χ1 PCA dynamic scalar (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn = 0.46), suggesting that, despite its inactivity, the ERBB3 pocket samples a limited subset of the conformational space explored by active kinases. The clustering result supports the view that ERBB3 functions primarily as a scaffold rather than a catalytic entity, a hypothesis previously proposed based on its inability to autophosphorylate and its role in heterodimeric signalling complexes [2].

In summary, the MD simulations demonstrate that ERBB3 maintains a tightly bound ATP ligand while allowing considerable flexibility in its activation loop, a combination that likely preserves a receptive conformation for regulatory interactions without enabling catalysis. Future work should interrogate the dynamics of the adjacent membrane‑proximal juxtamembrane region and the potential for allosteric modulation by heterodimeric partners, perhaps through targeted mutagenesis of key loop residues and subsequent simulation to evaluate changes in pocket stability and inter‑lobe communication.
