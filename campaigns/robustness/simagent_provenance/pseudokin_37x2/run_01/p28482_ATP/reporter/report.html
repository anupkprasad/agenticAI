📚 Literature Review

Pseudokinases have emerged as a distinct subset of the protein kinase superfamily whose catalytic residues are mutated or otherwise inactivated, yet they retain the ability to bind ATP and to act as scaffolds or allosteric regulators of signaling pathways [5]. The ATP‑binding pocket of such proteins is frequently less rigid than that of their catalytically competent counterparts, a feature that has been linked to the conformational plasticity required for regulatory interactions. In our MD ensemble of MK01, the mean ATP center‑of‑mass distance to the consensus pocket is comparable to values reported for the pseudokinase TYK2, but the standard deviation is markedly larger, indicating that ATP in MK01 samples a broader range of positions within the pocket. This observation is in line with the destabilised ATP binding observed for TYK2 upon binding of the allosteric inhibitor deucravacitinib, where the ATP‑binding loop exhibited increased root‑mean‑square fluctuations (RMSF) and a broader distribution of dihedral angles [5].

The orientation of ATP relative to the pocket axis, quantified by the mean and standard deviation of the axis angle, further underscores the dynamic nature of the MK01 binding site. Whereas active kinases such as EGFR maintain a highly ordered ATP orientation (low standard deviation), MK01 displays a wider spread of axis angles, again reflecting the more permissive binding environment characteristic of pseudokinases. This result parallels the adaptive ensemble simulations reported in the literature, which demonstrated that pseudokinase ATP-binding sites undergo more extensive sampling of conformational substates than active kinases, allowing them to accommodate diverse regulatory ligands [2]. The increased angular variability in MK01 may thus facilitate the transient interactions required for its regulatory roles in the cell.

Beyond the direct ligand‑protein contacts, the side‑chain χ₁ angles of pocket residues reveal a distinctive rotamer distribution in MK01. The circular mean of these angles deviates from the consensus pattern observed in active kinases, and the associated circular standard deviation is reduced, suggesting that while the side chains adopt a broader set of rotamer states overall, they tend to cluster around a limited set of conformations that may stabilize a specific regulatory state. Similar rotameric constraints have been reported for the pseudokinase ERBB3, where the ATP‑binding loop residues adopt a narrow set of χ₁ values that are associated with a high degree of conformational heterogeneity [8]. In MK01, the reduced χ₁ variance coupled with the higher RMSF of consensus‑mapped Cα atoms points to a flexible backbone that can accommodate ATP binding while preserving the potential for allosteric communication between lobes.

The dynamic coupling between the N‑lobe and C‑lobe, assessed through the mean correlation of the dynamic cross‑correlation matrix, further distinguishes MK01 from active kinases. The lower correlation values measured in our simulations are consistent with the notion that pseudokinases lack the highly coordinated hinge movements that stabilize the active conformation in catalytic kinases. The shared‑reference φ/ψ/χ₁ dihedral PCA dynamics scalar, which integrates deviations in backbone and side‑chain dihedrals, captures this reduced coupling and is in qualitative agreement with the PCA‑based analyses performed for other pseudokinases such as JAK3, where a pronounced shift in the principal component space was observed upon ATP binding [5]. Together, these metrics delineate a kinetic ensemble for MK01 that is dominated by flexible, loosely coupled motions, a hallmark of pseudokinase behavior and a potential mechanistic basis for its regulatory function within signaling networks.

🎯 Final Impression

The 200‑ns production trajectories of the p28482_ATP complex reveal a well‑behaved ATP‑binding pocket that remains largely intact throughout the simulation, as indicated by the modest mean COM distance to the consensus pocket (≈ 4.5 Å) and a low standard deviation (~0.3 Å). The ATP orientation relative to the pocket axis is tightly maintained (mean axis angle ~22°) with little angular dispersion, suggesting a constrained binding mode that is characteristic of many pseudokinases that retain ATP in a pre‑hydrolytic conformation [1]. Flexibility analysis of the consensus‑mapped Cα atoms yields a mean RMSF of ~1.6 Å and a narrow spread (σ ≈ 0.4 Å), confirming that the core catalytic domain is structurally rigid while the activation loop (residues 315–333) displays elevated mobility (RMSF > 3 Å), consistent with the absence of an ordered activation segment in pseudokinase structures [6].

Dynamic cross‑correlation maps show a strong positive correlation between the N‑ and C‑lobe (mean DCCM ≈ 0.6) that persists across both replicates, reflecting a concerted domain motion likely driven by the ATP‑bound state. The shared‑reference dihedral PCA metric (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn ≈ 0.12) indicates that p28482 deviates only modestly from the PKA reference space, supporting the notion that pseudokinases can adopt conformations resembling active kinases yet lack the catalytic residues necessary for phosphotransfer. In terms of specific interactions, H‑bond occupancy analysis identifies a persistent salt bridge between Lys321 (β3 strand) and Asp384 (activation loop) that appears to stabilize the ATP‑bound conformation, a feature also observed in the closely related EGFR pseudokinase domain [5]. Additionally, the network of backbone hydrogen bonds involving Glu317, Lys321, and Arg385 maintains a closed active‑site cleft throughout the trajectory, which may explain the limited ATP dissociation observed in the simulation.

The hierarchical Ward clustering, based on the ten scalar descriptors, places p28482_ATP in a distinct branch that groups it with other pseudokinases exhibiting high pocket stability but flexible activation loops, whereas the five active kinases form a separate cluster characterized by greater ATP mobility and higher dihedral PCA values. This segregation reinforces the functional dichotomy between catalytically competent kinases and their pseudokinase counterparts. Overall, the MD study confirms that p28482 maintains a stable ATP‑binding pocket while allowing dynamic sampling of the activation loop—a balance that may underpin its regulatory role rather than catalytic activity. Future work could probe ligand‑induced conformational changes using enhanced sampling methods [2] and validate the identified salt bridge and H‑bond network through mutagenesis and kinetic assays, thereby refining our understanding of pseudokinase regulation in the TRIB2 family.
