The orientation of ATP with respect to the pocket axis also displays a narrow distribution (mean ≈ 35°, SD ≈ 8°), indicating that the ribose‑phosphate backbone is consistently positioned to engage the hinge‑like residues that typically form the adenine‑binding motif in active kinases. This pattern is consistent with the “closed‑pocket” conformations captured in MD of the HER2 and SRC pseudokinases, where ATP remains tightly bound despite the absence of catalytic residues [4].

The RMSF profile of the consensus‑mapped Cα atoms further reinforces the notion of a comparatively stiff binding environment, with an average RMSF of 1.9 Å (SD = 0.4 Å). This is markedly lower than the 2.8 Å average observed in the fully active CDK2 and ERBB3 systems, suggesting that the structural scaffolding surrounding the ATP pocket is less pliable in the pseudokinase cohort. The N‑lobe ↔ C‑lobe DCCM analysis also shows a mean cross‑correlation of −0.12 in STK31, whereas active kinases typically display positive correlations (≈ 0.25). The relatively small displacement of STK31’s dihedral angles relative to KAPCA thus suggests that ATP binding does not merely “occupy” the pocket, but actively imposes a conformation that mimics a pre‑catalytic state, potentially priming the protein for regulatory interactions that are not captured in the current simulation timeframe.

Collectively, the quantitative descriptors derived from the STK31 simulations dovetail with the broader mechanistic insights gleaned from MD studies of both active kinases and pseudokinases. The restrained COM distances and tight orientation of ATP mirror the behavior of well‑characterized catalytic kinases, while the lower RMSF and negative inter‑lobe correlations align with the dynamic signatures of pseudokinases reported in the literature. These findings underscore the utility of comparative, consensus‑based metrics for interrogating ATP‑binding dynamics across kinase families and lay the groundwork for future investigations into the allosteric regulation and potential druggability of pseudokinases such as STK31.

Free‑energy landscape mapping uncovered two principal basins: a closed, ATP‑bound state and a partially open state where the activation loop (residues 210–230) is disordered and samples extended conformations. H‑bond occupancy analysis identifies persistent interactions between the ribose 2′‑OH and the side‑chain of Asp219, while a transient salt bridge between Lys142 and Glu219 stabilizes the closed basin. These structural features align with prior observations that pseudokinases maintain ATP in a bound but non‑productive state, modulating downstream signaling through conformational coupling [3].

In summary, the simulations confirm that q9bxu1_ATP occupies a stable ATP‑binding pocket yet retains a highly flexible activation loop, supporting its classification as a regulatory pseudokinase. Future work should probe the functional consequences of disrupting key pocket interactions (e.g., D219A or K142A) via mutagenesis and measure ATP affinity by isothermal titration calorimetry or NMR relaxation experiments.
