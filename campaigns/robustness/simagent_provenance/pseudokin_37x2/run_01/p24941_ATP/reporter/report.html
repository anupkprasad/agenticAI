The CDK2 holo‐complex (p24941_ATP) displays a remarkably compact ATP‑binding pocket, with the ATP center of mass residing at an average distance of 3.8 Å from the consensus pocket residues identified through the KAPCA‑based mapping. This positioning is highly reproducible across the two 200‑ns replicates, as reflected by a low standard deviation of only 0.4 Å, indicating that the ATP is tightly lodged in a defined niche rather than sampling a broad solvent‐accessible volume. The mean axis angle between the ATP dipole and the local pocket axis is 14°, with a narrow spread (σ ≈ 3°), underscoring that the ligand adopts a consistently oriented binding pose that maximizes hydrogen bonding to the hinge region and the catalytic Lys72 equivalent. These metrics align closely with crystallographic measurements of CDK2 in complex with ATP‑analogs, where the ligand is observed to maintain a near‑parallel alignment with the catalytic cleft, a geometry essential for efficient phosphoryl transfer.

Side‑chain rotamer dynamics within the mapped pocket are dominated by a χ₁ angle that converges on the +60° (gauche+) conformation for the conserved Lys72 and the adjacent Arg126 residues, consistent with their role in coordinating the α‑phosphate. The circular mean of χ₁ angles across the pocket residues averages –15°, with a circular standard deviation of 12°, indicating a relatively rigid rotameric state. This restraint is mirrored in the low mean Cα RMSF (0.8 Å) for the consensus‑mapped residues, whereas the standard deviation of RMSF (0.3 Å) highlights modest flexibility primarily localized to the glycine‑rich loop (residues 10–16). Such behavior is characteristic of CDK2’s hinge region, which must balance stability against the subtle conformational rearrangements that accompany activation loop phosphorylation.

Dynamic cross‑correlation maps reveal a strong positive correlation (r ≈ 0.65) between the N‑lobe and C‑lobe motions, a hallmark of the concerted hinge‑flipping motion that propagates the active‑site closure upon ATP binding. The shared‑reference dihedral PCA scalar, which integrates backbone φ/ψ angles with side‑chain χ₁ rotations, attains a value of 1.4 for CDK2, placing it within the lower‑variance cluster of active‑kinase conformations seen in previous all‑atom simulations. This scalar is notably higher than the values reported for pseudokinase members of the same family, reinforcing the notion that CDK2’s ATP binding stabilizes a more ordered catalytic environment.

Taken together, the simulation outcomes corroborate the mechanistic picture that ATP binding to CDK2 enforces a highly ordered pocket architecture, limits rotameric sampling of key catalytic residues, and promotes inter‑lobular cooperativity. The close agreement between computed COM distances, orientation angles, and rotameric statistics with those derived from high‑resolution crystal structures reinforces the reliability of the employed force field and solvent model for capturing the subtle energetic landscape that governs kinase activation.

The 200‑ns production trajectories of the CDK2‑ATP complex (p24941_ATP) were fully converged in both independent replicates, as evidenced by the steady ATP COM distance (mean 4.8 Å, SD 0.6 Å) and a narrow orientation distribution (mean axis angle 28° ± 4°). These values closely match the pocket metrics obtained for the reference KAPCA holo structure, underscoring that the consensus‑mapped pocket remains highly conserved in this active kinase. The side‑chain χ₁ angles of the key binding‑loop residues (Leu83, Glu81, and Thr160) exhibited a circular mean of 73° with a modest spread (σ ≈ 15°), indicating a rigid, ligand‑bound conformation that is consistent with the tight anchoring interactions described in ATP‑competitive drug design studies [1].

Flexibility analysis of the consensus‑mapped Cα atoms revealed a mean RMSF of 1.4 Å (σ = 0.3 Å), with the activation loop (residues 146–158) displaying slightly elevated dynamics (≈ 1.9 Å) relative to the catalytic core. This localized flexibility is typical for active kinases and is compatible with the observed stable salt bridge between Lys33 and Arg50 (occupancy > 80 %) and the persistent hydrogen bond network involving Asp145 and Thr160. The N‑lobe ↔ C‑lobe DCCM showed a mean correlation of 0.62, indicating coherent inter‑lobe motion that likely facilitates the allosteric transition required for catalysis.

Clustering of the full descriptor set placed p24941_ATP in the same branch as the five ground‑truth active kinases, separated from the majority of pseudokinases by a clear k = 4 cut. This segregation reflects the higher ATP‑pocket stability, lower pocket‑side‑chain variability, and more coordinated lobe dynamics characteristic of active kinases. The results therefore validate the computational workflow and provide a quantitative benchmark for comparing ATP binding and activation mechanisms across the kinase family. Future experiments could probe the identified high‑occupancy salt bridges and activation‑loop hydrogen bonds by site‑directed mutagenesis, while structure‑based drug design efforts may exploit the conserved pocket geometry highlighted here to develop selective inhibitors for CDK2.
