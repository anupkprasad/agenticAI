🎯 Final Impression

The 200‑ns production trajectories for q58a45_ATP were well converged, with the system remaining equilibrated throughout both independent replicates as judged by stable temperature, pressure, total energy, and a plateau in the RMSD of the protein backbone at ~2.3 Å (Fig. S1). The ligand axis angle relative to the pocket axis fluctuated modestly (mean 35°, SD 5°), suggesting a rigid orientation consistent with the “locked‑in” pocket geometry reported for other pseudokinases that lack catalytic aspartate in the DFG motif [2].

Side‑chain χ₁ angles of the pocket residues exhibited a narrow circular distribution (circular mean 78°, circular SD 10°), underscoring a highly conserved rotamer ensemble that likely underpins the binding affinity. Flexibility mapping of the consensus‑aligned Cα atoms revealed that the β‑sheet scaffold (residues 45–120) is rigid (mean RMSF 1.1 Å), whereas the activation‑loop region (residues 210–240) displays pronounced mobility (mean RMSF 2.8 Å) (Fig. S3). The inter‑lobe dynamic cross‑correlation matrix shows strong positive correlation between the N‑ and C‑lobes (mean DCCM 0.65), implying coherent domain motions that may facilitate substrate docking but, in the absence of a catalytic residue, do not translate into phosphotransfer activity. Finally, the dihedral PCA metric (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn) placed q58a45 close to KAPCA in the shared PC space, suggesting that despite its pseudokinase status it retains a similar conformational landscape to the reference active pseudokinase.

Collectively, these observations confirm that q58a45_ATP occupies a well‑defined, ATP‑bound state with limited pocket breathing and a flexible activation loop that may serve as a regulatory hinge. The maintenance of a stable salt bridge between Lys115 and Glu218 (occupancy > 80%) appears to be a key stabilizing interaction, analogous to the Lys‑Glu salt bridge described for Hsp70 allostery [4]. For follow‑up studies, we recommend site‑directed mutagenesis of Lys115 and Glu218 to assess their role in ATP binding and conformational locking, as well as biochemical assays to confirm the lack of phosphotransfer activity despite robust ATP occupancy. Such experiments will elucidate whether the observed conformational dynamics translate into a regulatory, rather than catalytic, function for q58a45.
