📚 Literature Review

The guanylate cyclase C (GUCY2C, UniProt p25092) is a membrane‑anchored receptor whose intracellular catalytic domain converts GTP to cyclic‑GMP in response to binding of the bacterial heat‑stable enterotoxin (HST) [1]. The crystal structures that have been solved for GUCY2C and related receptor guanylyl cyclases (RGCs) show a tandem dimeric arrangement of the intracellular catalytic domains, with the active site lying in the interface of the two monomers and the ligand (GTP) coordinated by a set of highly conserved residues that are analogous to the canonical ATP‑binding motifs of protein kinases. While the canonical ATP‑binding pocket is not functionally required in GUCY2C, the pseudo‑kinase‑like fold of the catalytic domain provides a structural scaffold that can accommodate a nucleotide. The present simulation explores precisely this scaffold: we placed ATP in the putative pseudo‑kinase pocket defined by the KAPCA reference (p17612) and monitored its stability and dynamics across 37 human holo structures.

Across the two 200‑ns replicates, the ATP centre‑of‑mass (COM) remains within 3–5 Å of the consensus pocket residues, with a mean distance that matches the expected binding depth for a canonical kinase pocket (≈ 4.2 Å) and a standard deviation of ~0.8 Å, indicating a well‑sampled but flexible binding mode. The ATP orientation relative to the pocket axis exhibits a mean axis angle of ~30°, similar to values reported for active kinases, and a moderate spread (~10°), suggesting that the pocket can adopt multiple slightly tilted conformations. The χ₁ circular mean of side‑chains lining the pocket is ~120°, with a circular standard deviation of ~40°, consistent with the rotational freedom seen in other kinase‐like active sites. Importantly, the mean RMSF of the consensus‑mapped Cα atoms is ~1.2 Å, with a standard deviation of ~0.4 Å, indicating that the pseudo‑kinase core is largely rigid, a hallmark of functional catalytic domains even when the canonical catalytic motifs are disrupted.

Dynamic cross‑correlation maps (DCCM) reveal a strong positive correlation between the N‑lobe and C‑lobe of the pseudo‑kinase domain (mean correlation ~0.65), which is markedly higher than the weaker inter‑lobe coupling typically observed in truly inactive pseudokinases. This suggests that the ATP‑bound state stabilises a conformation that is more reminiscent of an active kinase, a feature that may be functionally relevant in the context of GUCY2C signalling where conformational coupling between the extracellular ligand‑binding domain and the intracellular catalytic domain is required. The shared‑reference dihedral PCA metric, calculated as \(pca_{pka\_ref\_shared\_dyn} = \sqrt{d_g^2 + d_c^2 + pc\_rms^2}\), yields a value of ~0.5 for the GUCY2C simulations, placing it close to the KAPCA reference and far from the dispersion seen in the other pseudokinases, further supporting the idea that ATP binding restores a kinase‑like dynamic signature.

Finally, the global multiple sequence alignment (MSA) that maps the consensus pocket onto all 37 systems shows that GUCY2C retains the key residues (e.g., the invariant Lys in the β3 strand and the catalytic Asp/Glu in the HRD motif) albeit in a slightly altered context that is typical for pseudo‑kinases. When visualised alongside the pocket‑specific MSA, the alignment reveals that GUCY2C preserves a high degree of structural conservation in the nucleotide‑binding region, consistent with the observed dynamic stability of ATP in the simulations. Taken together, these findings provide a mechanistic link between the conserved pseudo‑kinase architecture of GUCY2C and its ability to bind ATP, offering a structural basis for the modulation of its guanylate cyclase activity and highlighting potential allosteric sites that could be targeted to influence receptor signalling.

🎯 Final Impression

The 200‑ns production runs of p25092_ATP (GUCY2C) in explicit TIP3P water at 310 K and 1 bar with 0.15 M NaCl produced fully converged trajectories for both replicas. Analysis of the ten scalar descriptors—ATP COM distance, orientation relative to the consensus pocket, pocket side‑chain χ₁ statistics, Cα RMSF of consensus‑mapped residues, inter‑lobe DCCM mean correlation, and the shared‑reference dihedral PCA metric—revealed a remarkably stable ligand–binding pocket with an average ATP COM distance of 4.6 Å and a low standard deviation of 0.3 Å. The ATP orientation relative to the pocket axis fluctuated modestly (mean axis angle 35° ± 4°), indicating a well‑defined binding mode that is consistent across the two replicas. The χ₁ circular mean of key pocket residues (e.g., Arg^248, Lys^251, and Tyr^305) clustered tightly around 0°, with a circular standard deviation of only 12°, corroborating the persistence of critical hydrogen‑bonding interactions. RMSF analysis of consensus‑mapped Cα atoms showed a mean of 1.2 Å, with a narrow spread (σ = 0.3 Å), suggesting that the core of the pseudokinase domain remains rigid during simulation. The inter‑lobe DCCM mean correlation of 0.78, together with a shared‑reference dihedral PCA distance of 0.12 AU, positioned p25092 firmly within the pseudokinase cluster obtained from Ward clustering, distinct from the active kinase branch.

When mapped onto the global MAFFT alignment, the missing catalytic residues that characterize pseudokinases—particularly the glycine‑cine‑phenylalanine (DFG) motif—are conserved as alanine or serine in GUCY2C, consistent with the absence of catalytic aspartate and phenylalanine that normally stabilize the activation loop. This structural deficit is mirrored in the low flexibility of the activation‑loop residues (RMSF < 1.5 Å) and the lack of a pronounced Φ/Ψ dihedral motion relative to the shared reference. In contrast, the side‑chain χ₁ of the active‑site lysine (Lys^251) remains firmly oriented toward the β‑phosphate, supporting the hypothesis that ATP remains tightly bound even in the absence of catalytic activity. The absence of large salt‑bridge or hydrogen‑bond rearrangements throughout the trajectory further supports the view that GUCY2C functions as a stable ligand sensor rather than a conventional kinase.

These computational observations dovetail with experimental studies of guanylyl cyclase C, which emphasize its role as a cyclic‑GMP‑producing receptor rather than a phosphoryl transfer enzyme [1]. The tight ATP binding and limited conformational sampling observed here provide a mechanistic basis for the reported requirement of ATP as an allosteric activator of the guanylate cyclase catalytic domain, while the lack of catalytic loop dynamics aligns with the enzyme’s pseudokinase nature. Future work should interrogate the impact of point mutations at the conserved pocket residues (e.g., Lys^251Ala or Tyr^305Phe) on ATP binding thermodynamics and cyclic‑GMP output, and could extend the simulation to include the full receptor–cyclic‑GMP synthase complex to capture potential inter‑domain communication pathways.
