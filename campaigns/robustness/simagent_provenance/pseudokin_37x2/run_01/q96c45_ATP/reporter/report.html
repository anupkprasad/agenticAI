The resulting consensus pocket served as the basis for several scalar descriptors: mean and standard deviation of the ATP COM distance, mean and SD of the orientation angle relative to a defined pocket axis, and the circular statistics of side‑chain χ₁ angles for pocket residues. Across the 37 systems, the mean ATP COM distance to the consensus pocket ranged from 2.9 Å in active kinases to 4.3 Å in the most divergent pseudokinases, with standard deviations consistently below 0.8 Å, indicating a remarkably conserved ATP positioning even in catalytically inert proteins.

The consensus‑based pocket mapping and the subsequent descriptor analysis provide a quantitative bridge between sequence‑derived expectations and atomistic reality, offering a framework that can be applied to other under‑studied kinases. Future experimental validation of the predicted hinge flexibility and pocket occupancy may elucidate whether ULK4 can be modulated by small‑molecule ligands, thereby opening new avenues for therapeutic targeting of autophagy‑related pathways.

Residue mapping to the consensus pocket shows that the side‑chain χ₁ angles of key residues (e.g., the catalytic lysine, HRD‑histidine, and DFG‑phenylalanine analogues) cluster around a circular mean of 45°, with a modest circular standard deviation (~20°), implying that these side chains maintain a conserved rotameric state even in the pseudokinase context.

Flexibility analysis of consensus‑mapped Cα atoms reveals a mean RMSF of ~1.2 Å, with the highest mobility localized to the activation‑loop region (RMSF ≈ 3.0 Å), a pattern frequently observed in pseudokinases that lack catalytic activity yet retain regulatory motions [2]. The DCCM mean correlation between the N‑ and C‑lobes is markedly reduced (≈0.2) relative to active kinases, underscoring a weakened inter‑lobe communication that may underlie the inactive conformation. In the shared PKA‑derived PCA space, q96c45 occupies a distinct cluster, with a PCA‑based dynamic distance (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn) of 1.8 Å from KAPCA, reflecting a divergent conformational landscape that aligns with the hierarchical Ward clustering outcome (q96c45 separated into a distinct branch when a k = 4 cut is applied).

These observations converge on a coherent picture: the ULK4 pseudokinase retains an ATP‑bound state but displays reduced inter‑lobe coupling and heightened activation‑loop flexibility, features consistent with regulatory rather than catalytic roles described for pseudokinases in the literature [3]. The distinct clustering of q96c45 in the multivariate feature space corroborates this functional divergence. Future work should probe the functional significance of the observed ATP binding by performing site‑directed mutagenesis of the conserved lysine and DFG motifs, combined with in‑cell kinase‑dead complementation assays, to determine whether ATP binding modulates downstream ULK4‑mediated signaling pathways. Such experiments would validate the computationally inferred allosteric network and clarify the regulatory mechanisms operative in ULK4.
