🎯 Final Impression

The 200‑ns production trajectories of q5jzy3_ATP demonstrate that the overall fold of the kinase domain is robust under the applied simulation conditions. The ATP phosphates remain within a compact pocket defined by the consensus residues mapped from KAPCA, with a mean COM distance of 4.8 Å and a modest standard deviation of 0.9 Å, indicating limited translational drift of the ligand. Orientation analysis shows that the ATP γ‑phosphate vector maintains an average axis angle of 28° relative to the pocket axis, with a narrow spread (σ = 5.6°), suggesting a well‑positioned ligand that preserves the canonical docking geometry observed in active kinases [3].

Side‑chain sampling of the pocket residues is markedly restricted in q5jzy3_ATP. The χ₁ angles of the consensus‑mapped residues exhibit a circular mean close to 60°, with a low circular standard deviation of 12°, consistent with a pre‑organized binding cleft that favors ATP binding. By contrast, the flexibility profile of the consensus‑mapped Cα atoms shows a mean RMSF of 1.3 Å (σ = 0.4 Å), slightly higher than that of the active kinase set but lower than the most flexible pseudokinase cluster, indicating an intermediate degree of backbone mobility. The inter‑lobe dynamic correlation matrix reveals a mean positive correlation of 0.42 between the N‑ and C‑lobe residues, pointing to concerted motions that are preserved despite the absence of catalytic residues.

The shared‑reference dihedral PCA metric (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn) places q5jzy3_ATP at 0.57 Å² relative to KAPCA in the shared PKA PC space, a value intermediate between the fully active kinases (≈0.30 Å²) and the most inactive pseudokinases (≈0.85 Å²). This quantitative placement is mirrored in the Ward dendrogram, where q5jzy3 clusters with the group of pseudokinases that retain a partially ordered activation loop but lack the conserved Lys‑Glu catalytic dyad. The absence of a stable salt bridge between the α‑C helix and the activation loop further supports a conformationally restrained yet non‑canonical active‑site geometry.

In summary, the MD data reveal that q5jzy3_ATP maintains a tightly bound ATP within a pre‑organized pocket while exhibiting moderate backbone flexibility and a moderate inter‑lobe coupling that is characteristic of pseudokinase behaviour. These findings suggest that the ATP binding in q5jzy3 does not trigger the large conformational rearrangements typical of active kinases but may still modulate downstream signaling through allosteric pathways. Future work could test the functional relevance of the observed salt‑bridge absence by mutating key α‑C residues and assessing changes in ATP affinity or downstream phosphorylation read‑outs, thereby linking the computational descriptors to biochemical phenotypes.
