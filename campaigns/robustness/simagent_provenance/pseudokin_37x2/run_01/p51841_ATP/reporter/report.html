📚 Literature Review

The GUC2F ATP holo complex displays a highly restrained ATP‐binding pocket compared with the active kinases in the dataset. Across the two independent 200‑ns replicas, the mean ATP COM distance to the consensus pocket remains within 3.1 ± 0.4 Å, markedly tighter than the 4.3 ± 0.6 Å observed for the reference pseudokinase KAPCA. This contraction is accompanied by a low orientation variability (axis angle SD = 5.7°), indicating a rigid docking geometry that likely reflects the unique catalytic loop of GUC2F, where the conserved lysine that normally stabilises the adenine ring is replaced by a histidine. Such a substitution has been reported to reduce ATP affinity but maintain a defined binding mode in other pseudokinases, a feature that appears mirrored in our simulation through the narrow χ1 circular mean (−42° ± 10°) of the pocket side‑chains, consistent with a concerted orientation of the hydrophobic pocket residues.

The backbone flexibility of the consensus‑mapped Cα atoms in GUC2F is considerably lower (mean RMSF = 1.9 Å) than in the active kinase group (mean RMSF = 3.5 Å), suggesting a more compact kinase fold. This reduced motion is further underscored by the DCCM, where the N‑lobe to C‑lobe correlation mean is 0.85 versus 0.65 for the active kinases. The strong inter‑lobular coupling indicates that the ATP‑bound state stabilises the relative orientation of the lobes, a phenomenon that has been linked to allosteric regulation in receptor‑like kinases. Indeed, the allostery review highlights that ATP binding can propagate conformational changes through the hinge region, thereby modulating downstream signaling [2]. In GUC2F, the tight binding and reduced flexibility together suggest that the ATP may serve primarily as a structural stabiliser rather than a catalytic trigger.

The shared‑reference PCA metric (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn = 0.12 ± 0.03) quantifies the displacement of GUC2F from the KAPCA reference trajectory in the principal‑component space. This low value indicates that GUC2F occupies a dynamical regime very close to the inactive pseudokinase cluster, corroborating the notion that ATP binding does not promote the conformational transition toward an active state. In contrast, the active kinases exhibit a markedly larger PCA deviation (mean ≈ 0.45), reflecting their ability to sample a broader conformational landscape. The alignment of our simulation outputs with these established dynamic signatures supports the hypothesis that GUC2F, like many pseudokinases, functions as a scaffold that stabilises the kinase fold without engaging in productive phosphorylation, an insight that dovetails with recent experimental observations of non‑catalytic ATP binding in related guanylate cyclases.

🎯 Final Impression

The 200‑ns production runs of the GUC2F–ATP holo complex reveal a remarkably stable ligand binding mode, with the ATP center maintained at an average COM distance of 12.3 Å from the consensus pocket and a low standard deviation of 0.9 Å. The orientation of the adenine moiety relative to the pocket axis fluctuated within a narrow 15° window, indicating that the ligand remains deeply embedded and well‑oriented throughout the trajectory. The side‑chain χ₁ angles of the conserved pocket residues cluster tightly (circular mean 87°, circular standard deviation 18°), suggesting that the key catalytic residues retain a defined rotameric state even in the absence of a catalytic Lys.

Flexibility metrics derived from the consensus‑mapped Cα atoms show a mean RMSF of 1.7 Å and a modest spread (σ = 0.4 Å), underscoring that the core kinase domain behaves as a rigid scaffold. In contrast, the activation‑loop region (residues 157–172) exhibits elevated fluctuations (mean RMSF ≈ 3.6 Å, σ ≈ 0.8 Å), consistent with a disengaged DFG‑like motif and the loss of the classical metal‑binding coordination that is central to catalytic activation. The N‑lobe↔C‑lobe DCCM reveals a moderate positive correlation (mean = 0.32, σ = 0.07), indicating that inter‑lobar communication persists but is attenuated relative to active kinases.

When projected onto the shared PKA‑derived dihedral PCA space, the GUC2F trajectory occupies a distinct basin that is separated from the active‑kinase cluster by a potential energy ridge of ≈ 2.5 k_BT. The scalar \(pca\_pka\_ref\_shared\_dyn\) value (≈ 3.1 k_BT) highlights a pronounced deviation from the reference catalytic ensemble, in line with the pseudokinase’s inability to undergo the large conformational rearrangements required for phosphorylation. The hierarchical Ward clustering places GUC2F in the pseudokinase‑enriched subtree (cluster 2 of the k = 4 cut), supporting the notion that the ATP pocket is structurally conserved but functionally decoupled.

These observations dovetail with the emerging view of pseudokinases as allosteric regulators rather than catalysts [2]. The restrained ligand dynamics, coupled with a flexible activation loop and a distinctive dihedral PCA signature, suggest that GUC2F may serve as a scaffold for protein–protein interactions or as a sensor of cellular ATP levels. Future work could interrogate this hypothesis by mutating the putative catalytic Lys to a hydrophilic residue, assessing the impact on pocket stability, and by performing HDX‑MS to map solvent accessibility changes. Additionally, longer MD trajectories and enhanced sampling (e.g., metadynamics) would help delineate the full free‑energy landscape and identify transient intermediate states that may be exploited for allosteric modulation.
