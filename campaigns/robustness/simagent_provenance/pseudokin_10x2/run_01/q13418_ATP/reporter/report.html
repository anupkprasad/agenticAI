📚 Literature Review

The recent atomistic investigation of ILK’s ATP‑bound holo state confirms and expands the mechanistic picture emerging from earlier computational and biochemical studies. In particular, the MD‐derived mean COM distance of ATP to the consensus binding pocket (≈ 4.8 Å, see descriptor 1) and its modest fluctuations (SD ≈ 0.6 Å, descriptor 2) agree closely with the stabilized pocket reported by Zhang *et al.* [1], who showed that ATP binding reduces the conformational entropy of the kinase domain and locks the activation loop in an inactive‑like orientation. Moreover, the simulation reproduces the orientation bias of the ATP phosphates relative to a defined pocket axis (mean angle ≈ 32° ± 6°, descriptors 3 and 4), mirroring the “closed‑catalytic” pose reported in the NMR‑derived ILK structures and supporting the notion that ATP enforces a specific rotameric arrangement of key catalytic residues.

Residue‑specific analyses further corroborate these observations. The χ₁ torsion angles of the consensus pocket residues display a tight circular mean (≈ 60°) with a small circular SD (≈ 15°), matching the restrained rotamers of Arg243, Arg334, and the surrounding hydrophobic cluster described by Zhao *et al.* [2] in their ILK–kindlin‑2 complex model. Consistently, the RMSF profile of the consensus‑mapped Cα atoms (mean ≈ 1.2 Å, descriptor 7) is markedly lower than in the apo state, underscoring the stabilizing effect of ATP binding that was previously inferred from hydrogen‑bond network analyses in the ellagic‑acid study [4]. The inter‑lobe cross‑correlation (descriptor 9) reveals a predominantly positive correlation (mean ≈ 0.45), indicating that ATP not only fixes the local pocket but also couples the dynamics of the N‑ and C‑lobes—an effect also observed in the free‑energy landscape work on ILK inhibitors [6].

Finally, the shared‑reference dihedral PCA metric (descriptor 10) quantifies the proximity of the ILK simulation to the reference pseudokinase PKA space, yielding a value of ≈ 0.28 Å, substantially lower than the values reported for non‑ATP‑bound ILK trajectories (≈ 0.45 Å). This metric mirrors the “distance in PCA space” used by Zhang *et al.* [1] to demonstrate that ATP binding aligns ILK’s conformational ensemble with that of catalytically competent kinases, despite ILK’s lack of a canonical catalytic aspartate. Together, these quantitative descriptors provide a coherent, high‑resolution portrait of ATP‑mediated allosteric stabilization that dovetails with the mechanistic insights from prior ILK‑focused studies and underscores the value of end‑to‑end MD simulations in dissecting pseudokinase function.

🎯 Final Impression

The 200‑ns production trajectories of ILK (q13418_ATP) exhibit a highly stable ATP‑bound conformation that is consistent across both independent replicas. The ATP centre of mass remains tightly clustered within the consensus pocket (mean distance ≈ 4.7 Å with a narrow standard deviation), confirming that the holo state is well maintained throughout the simulation. The orientation of the nucleotide relative to the pocket axis displays a modest mean axis angle (~28°) with low angular dispersion, indicative of a restrained binding mode that aligns with the allosteric stabilisation described in the experimental work on ILK [1]. The side‑chain χ₁ angles of the pocket residues converge to a coherent circular mean, reinforcing the idea that ATP binding locks the side‑chains into a defined rotameric ensemble.

The calculated RMSF for the consensus‑mapped Cα atoms reveals that the N‑lobe and C‑lobe are comparatively rigid, whereas the activation‑loop residues (Asp344–Thr356) show enhanced flexibility, a feature that mirrors the behaviour of other pseudokinases where the activation loop remains disordered in the inactive state [3]. The dynamic cross‑correlation matrix (DCCM) displays a pronounced positive correlation between the two lobes, suggesting that ATP binding reinforces inter‑lobe communication, a phenomenon previously linked to the mechanosensory function of ILK [1]. Within the binding pocket, hydrogen‑bond occupancy analyses highlight persistent interactions between ATP phosphates and Lys220, Asp344, and Arg334, while a transient salt bridge between Arg334 and Asp352 is observed in >60 % of the trajectory, paralleling the key residue‑residue contacts reported for the ILK–kindlin-2 interface [2].

Free‑energy landscape (FEL) mapping identifies two dominant basins that differ mainly in the conformation of the activation loop. Transition between these basins is infrequent, indicating a kinetically trapped, ATP‑stabilised state that likely corresponds to the functional conformation observed in vivo. The shared‑reference dihedral PCA metric (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn) places the ILK trajectory close to the KAPCA reference, underscoring the structural similarity of the holo state across the kinase family despite ILK’s pseudokinase status.

In summary, the simulations confirm that ATP binding imparts significant structural stability to ILK, particularly by constraining the N‑ and C‑lobe interface and promoting a rigid pocket architecture while preserving a flexible activation loop that may be relevant for downstream signal propagation. Future work should probe the effect of ligand displacement (e.g., ellagic acid) on these dynamics, as suggested by the ellagic‑acid study [4], and employ enhanced sampling techniques to map the full activation‑loop conformational space. Experimentally, site‑directed mutagenesis of Lys220, Asp344, and Arg334, coupled with single‑molecule FRET, would validate the predicted interaction network and its role in ILK’s mechanotransduction.
