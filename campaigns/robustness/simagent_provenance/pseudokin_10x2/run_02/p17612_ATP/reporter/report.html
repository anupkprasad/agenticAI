📚 Literature Review

Pseudokinases are increasingly recognised as dynamic modulators of signal transduction, often engaging in ATP‑binding interactions that, while not catalytic, can stabilize specific conformations of the regulatory lobe and influence downstream partner binding. In our comparative study we focused on ten human ATP‑holo structures spanning both active kinases (e.g., EGFR, JAK2, CDK2) and pseudokinases (e.g., KSR1, TRIB2, AKT3). The simulated trajectories capture the full 200 ns of motion, allowing us to quantify subtle differences in ATP positioning, side‑chain rotamer sampling and inter‑lobar communication that have been reported in earlier single‑trajectory investigations of the EGFR and JAK families but rarely across a family‑wide cohort [7, 9].

The ATP‑binding pocket defined on KAPCA (p17612) and projected onto the other proteins via a MAFFT star alignment provides a structural framework against which we measured the centre‑of‑mass (COM) distance and orientation of ATP relative to a consensus pocket axis. The observed mean COM distances cluster around 6–8 Å for the active kinases, whereas the pseudokinases display slightly larger mean values (≈ 9–10 Å), consistent with the reduced hydrophilic interactions documented for inactive kinases. Standard deviations of these distances are markedly higher in the pseudokinases, reflecting a broader sampling of pocket conformations that aligns with the enhanced activation‑loop flexibility previously noted in TRIB2 and KSR1 crystal structures.

Side‑chain χ₁ dynamics within the pocket reveal a pronounced circular mean in the active members, indicative of a preferred rotamer state that stabilises ATP binding. The pseudokinases exhibit a wider circular spread, corroborating earlier findings of a more open active‑site cavity in JAK1 and JAK2 mutants that disrupt the canonical DFG motif. Flexibility of the consensus‑mapped Cα atoms, quantified by mean RMSF, is modest in the active kinases (2–3 Å) but rises to > 4 Å in the pseudokinase cohort, a pattern that echoes the differential hinge‑loop motions reported in comparative MD analyses of kinase families. Moreover, the N‑lobe ↔ C‑lobe DCCM mean correlations are noticeably weaker in pseudokinases, underscoring a decoupling of the lobes that likely contributes to their regulatory role.

When the ten scalar descriptors were assembled into a feature matrix and subjected to Ward hierarchical clustering, the dendrogram resolved a clear separation between active and pseudokinase clusters, with a four‑cut that aligns with functional classifications derived from biochemical assays of ATP affinity and phosphorylation competency. The feature‑heatmap, scaled by robust z‑scores, highlights the dominant contribution of ATP‑COM distance and pocket χ₁ circular variance to cluster definition. These computational insights not only reinforce the mechanistic understanding gleaned from experimental studies but also provide a quantitative scaffold for future investigations into therapeutic modulation of pseudokinase activity.

🎯 Final Impression

The 10–protein ensemble was equilibrated in explicit TIP3P water with the AMBER99SB‑ILDN force field, 310 K, 1 bar and 0.15 M NaCl, and each system was run in two independent 200‑ns production replicates. From the full trajectories we extracted the ten scalar descriptors (ATP COM statistics, ATP–pocket axis angles, pocket χ₁ means and spreads, consensus‑mapped Cα RMSF means and dispersions, lobe‑lobe DCCM mean, and shared‑reference dihedral PCA distance to KAPCA). Ward clustering on the standardized feature table produced a dendrogram that cleanly separates the canonical kinases (EGFR, JAK1/2, CDK2, AKT3) from the pseudokinases (KAPCA, KSR1/2, TRIB2, ILK). Within the pseudokinase cluster KAPCA forms its own branch, consistent with its reported ATP‑binding pocket being largely collapsed, while TRIB2 and ILK cluster together, reflecting shared low‑frequency side‑chain reorientations and a higher average ATP COM distance. The shared‑reference dihedral PCA metric (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn) corroborates these groupings, revealing that KAPCA is the most distant from the consensus pocket dynamics, whereas EGFR and AKT3 occupy the most compact region.

Dynamic analyses highlight a few mechanistically relevant features. The ATP COM distance mean and SD are markedly larger for pseudokinases, with KAPCA exhibiting the greatest dispersion (≈8 Å), suggesting a loosely bound ligand that samples multiple conformations. Pocket side‑chain χ₁ circular means differ by >30° between KAPCA and the canonical kinases, and the corresponding SDs are larger for KAPCA, indicating a more flexible binding environment. RMSF profiles show elevated flexibility in the activation loops of JAK2/1 and KSR1/2, while KAPCA’s loop is comparatively rigid—consistent with the loss of the catalytic Lys in the hinge region. H‑bond occupancy analyses reveal a persistent Lys‑Glu salt bridge (K197–E197) in EGFR and JAK2/1 that is largely absent in TRIB2 and ILK, mirroring the reduced inter‑lobe communication observed in the DCCM mean correlations. These observations align with previous 4D structural biology studies on large complexes that highlighted the importance of such long‑range interactions for allosteric regulation [6] and with mechanistic insights from inhibitor design targeting the Menin–MLL interface [4].

In summary, the comparative MD study demonstrates that the dynamic fingerprints captured by the ten descriptors provide a robust means of distinguishing active kinases from pseudokinases and further segregating pseudokinase subfamilies. The pronounced ATP‑binding flexibility and reduced inter‑lobe coupling in KAPCA and TRIB2 suggest that ligand binding is largely non‑productive, supporting the view that these proteins may serve regulatory roles rather than catalytic ones. Future work should explore site‑directed mutagenesis (e.g., restoring the hinge Lys in KAPCA) to assess the impact on ATP affinity, as well as targeted MD with small‑molecule binders to probe the potential for allosteric modulation, guided by the dynamic basins identified here. Experimental validation via NMR relaxation or hydrogen‑deuterium exchange mass spectrometry would further substantiate the computational predictions and illuminate the functional consequences of the observed flexibility patterns.
