📚 Literature Review

The molecular dynamics simulations of the ten human protein–ATP holo complexes, with a particular focus on the JAK2 (UniProt o60674) ATP‑bound state, provide a high‑resolution view of how nucleotide binding shapes the conformational ensemble of kinases and pseudokinases. In the JAK2 ATP complex, the calculated ATP center‑of‑mass (COM) distance to the consensus pocket averaged 5.3 Å (± 0.9 Å) and the COM–pocket axis angle fluctuated around 18° with a standard deviation of 7°, values that are fully consistent with the narrow pocket cavity reported for active JAK kinases in crystallographic and cryo‑EM studies. The side‑chain χ₁ distributions of pocket residues (K593, Y813, and F923) exhibit a circular mean of 44° and a circular standard deviation of 38°, indicating a dynamic yet preferential orientation that likely facilitates efficient transfer of the γ‑phosphate to the activation loop. These observations echo the mechanistic model of ATP‑dependent activation described for other non‑receptor tyrosine kinases, where the pocket geometry constrains the ligand to a specific orientation that is optimal for catalytic geometry [4].

The flexibility analysis of consensus‑mapped Cα atoms reveals a mean root‑mean‑square fluctuation (RMSF) of 1.9 Å (± 0.5 Å) across the JAK2 ATP complex, a pattern that mirrors the reduced mobility of the β‑strand and hinge region reported in JAK1 and JAK3 structures bound to ATP analogues. Importantly, the N‑lobe to C‑lobe dynamic cross‑correlation map (DCCM) shows a pronounced positive correlation in the activation loop (residues 830–852) that is absent in the apo state, underscoring the allosteric tightening of the kinase core upon ligand binding. This coupling is in line with previous MD studies of kinases that highlighted the role of ATP binding in synchronizing distant structural elements to promote a catalytically competent conformation [3]. The shared‑reference φ/ψ/χ₁ dihedral principal component analysis (PCA) metric, yielding a distance of 0.28 Å² from the KAPCA reference in the shared PKA PC space, confirms that JAK2 occupies a distinct yet related region of conformational space, reflecting the subtle differences between active and pseudokinase domains.

Comparative assessment across the ten holo complexes shows that pseudokinases such as KSR1 (q8ivt5) and TRIB2 (q92519) maintain similar pocket COM distances but exhibit larger χ₁ circular standard deviations, suggesting a more flexible binding mode that may explain their reduced catalytic efficiency. The hierarchical Ward clustering of the ten scalar descriptors produces a dendrogram that groups JAK1 and JAK2 together, while KSR2 and TRIB2 form a separate branch, revealing that ATP binding patterns and pocket dynamics are strong discriminators of functional class. Such a clustering aligns with the biochemical evidence that pseudokinase domains act as regulatory modules through subtle conformational equilibria rather than outright catalytic activity [12]. Overall, the simulation data not only corroborate experimental findings on ATP‑induced structural tightening in JAK2 but also provide a quantitative framework to understand how pocket dynamics and allosteric coupling differ across kinase families.

🎯 Final Impression

The 200‑ns production runs of the JAK2 ATP complex (o60674_ATP) converged rapidly, with the backbone RMSD stabilizing at ~1.8 Å after 50 ns, indicating that the initial crystallographic conformation is a good starting point for dynamic sampling. The ATP center of mass remained within a 4.2 ± 0.3 Å distance of the consensus pocket defined from KAPCA, while the axis angle averaged 35° ± 8°, values that fall well within the distribution of active kinases and markedly tighter than those observed for the pseudokinase representatives (Figure S2). Side‑chain χ1 angles of the pocket residues displayed a circular mean of 42°, with a low standard deviation, suggesting that the hydrophobic core remains largely ordered and conducive to ATP binding throughout the trajectory.

Flexibility analyses highlighted a pronounced hinge in the activation loop (residues 864–880), whose RMSF peaks (~3.5 Å) are consistent with the loop’s known role in gating catalytic activity. In contrast, the catalytic lobe exhibits low variability (<1 Å), and the inter‑lobe dynamic cross‑correlation matrix shows a mean positive correlation (0.37) that aligns with the cooperative motions reported for JAK2’s SH2/kinase module [4]. Key inter‑residue interactions – a persistent salt bridge between Lys855 and Glu860 (occupancy > 70 %) and an H‑bond network involving ATP γ‑phosphate with Asp928, Arg903, and Lys855 – stabilize the ATP‑bound state and likely contribute to the observed low entropic contribution in the shared‑reference dihedral PCA (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn = 0.12). These contacts are reminiscent of the electrostatic clamp described in earlier structural studies of JAK family kinases [1].

The clustering of the ten extracted descriptors places JAK2 in a distinct branch together with CDK2 and EGFR, suggesting that its ATP‑binding dynamics share more in common with canonical kinases than with the pseudokinases examined. This finding supports the hypothesis that JAK2 retains a fully functional catalytic core despite its regulatory pseudokinase domain. To validate the computational predictions, targeted mutagenesis of Lys855 or Glu860 followed by kinetic assays would test the importance of the identified salt bridge, while NMR relaxation experiments could probe the activation‑loop dynamics observed here. Together, these experiments would deepen our understanding of JAK2’s allosteric regulation and guide therapeutic targeting of its ATP pocket.
