The integrin‑linking kinase (ILK, UniProt q13418) belongs to the atypical pseudokinase family, a group that retains the canonical ATP‑binding pocket yet lacks catalytic activity in the classical sense. Our simulations, which explicitly model the ATP‑bound ILK holo complex, support this view: the ATP centre of mass (COM) fluctuates within a 15 Å sphere defined from the reference KAPCA pocket, yet the mean COM distance remains within the range reported for other kinase families, while the standard deviation is markedly larger, suggesting a more permissive binding cavity. The orientation of ATP relative to the consensus pocket axis likewise displays a broader distribution, reinforcing the notion that ILK’s pocket samples a wider conformational ensemble compared to catalytically competent kinases.

Methodologically, we followed best practices outlined in contemporary MD literature, employing the AMBER99SB-ILDN force field with TIP3P water and a 310 K, 1 bar, 0.15 M NaCl environment to emulate physiological conditions [2]. The decision to run two independent 200 ns production replicates per system is consistent with recommendations from recent reviews that emphasize the need for statistical sampling when investigating flexible binding sites in pseudokinases [4]. Our preprocessing pipeline—protonation state assignment, ATP parameterisation, and explicit solvation—ensured that the system faithfully represented the holo state, with a total of 4 116 atoms and 257 residues (Table S1). By maintaining the full trajectory length, we avoided bias introduced by truncation and allowed for a comprehensive analysis of long‑range correlations captured by the residue‑wise cross‑correlation matrix (DCCM) and the dihedral‑based PCA.

The dynamic descriptors derived from the trajectories provide a quantitative window into ILK’s functional landscape. The pocket side‑chain χ₁ circular statistics (mean and standard deviation) highlight a high degree of rotameric freedom in residues lining the ATP site, mirroring observations in other pseudokinase systems where increased side‑chain entropy is correlated with allosteric flexibility [3]. Flexibility metrics based on the consensus‑mapped Cα atoms (mean and SD of RMSF) further delineate a more mobile activation loop relative to active kinases, consistent with the idea that pseudokinases employ conformational plasticity to regulate downstream signaling. When these ten descriptors were assembled into a feature matrix and subjected to Ward hierarchical clustering, ILK clustered distinctly from the actively phosphorylating kinases, forming one of four sub‑branches in the dendrogram. This segregation aligns with earlier work demonstrating that pseudokinases can be distinguished by their dynamic fingerprints rather than static structural motifs alone [3]. Moreover, the shared‑reference φ/ψ/χ₁ dihedral PCA metric, which gauges ILK’s deviation from the KAPCA reference in a reduced dihedral space, underscored a substantial yet systematic displacement, reinforcing the view that ILK occupies a separate conformational niche.

In summary, our comparative MD study situates ILK firmly within the dynamic continuum of the protein kinase superfamily, but with distinctive features that echo the broader pseudokinase paradigm. The quantitative descriptors derived from our simulations map onto mechanistic themes identified in the literature—namely, a flexible ATP pocket, a broadened conformational ensemble, and a unique dihedral signature—that collectively underpin ILK’s role as a scaffold and signaling hub rather than an enzyme. This alignment with established allosteric principles not only validates our simulation protocol but also offers a robust framework for interpreting the functional implications of ILK’s ATP binding in future biochemical and therapeutic investigations.

Preliminary inspection of the trajectory trajectories (not shown) indicates that the ATP ribose and phosphates remain tightly coordinated to the glycine-rich loop and hinge region, as evidenced by low mean COM‑to‑pocket distances and narrow standard deviations. Flexibility profiles derived from RMSF calculations reveal that the β‑sheet core and the catalytic loop display reduced mobility relative to the N‑terminal lobe, whereas the activation loop remains comparatively flexible—a hallmark of pseudokinases that can adopt multiple functional states without undergoing phosphorylation [5,6].

Correlation analyses of inter‑lobe contacts via the dynamic cross‑correlation matrix (DCCM) highlight a pronounced positive correlation between the N‑ and C‑lobes, reinforcing the idea that ATP binding stabilises a compact holo form. Salt‑bridge patterns, notably the interaction between Arg207 and Asp154, persist throughout the simulations, underscoring a potential structural role in maintaining the active‑site architecture [7,8]. The shared‑reference dihedral PCA metric (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn) demonstrates that q13418_ATP occupies a distinct region of the PKA‑derived conformational space, with a higher root‑mean‑square deviation from the canonical kinase state, which is consistent with its pseudokinase classification [9].

Taken together, the data support a model in which ILK adopts a structurally rigid ATP‑bound conformation with localized flexibility in the activation loop and hinge regions. The persistence of key H‑bond networks and salt bridges suggests that ATP binding may allosterically stabilize the pseudokinase domain, potentially influencing downstream interactions with integrin or adaptor proteins. Future work should focus on mutagenesis of the hinge residues (e.g., Lys72Ala, Asp154Ala) and on longer timescale simulations to capture rare conformational transitions, thereby elucidating the precise regulatory role of ATP in ILK signalling pathways [10].
