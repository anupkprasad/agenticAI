TRIB2 belongs to the Tribbles subfamily of pseudokinases, which lack the canonical catalytic aspartate and lysine residues required for phosphotransfer yet retain a structurally conserved ATP‑binding cleft. Several biochemical studies have demonstrated that TRIB2 can bind ATP, and that this occupancy stabilises interactions with MAPK signalling components [3]. Importantly, the ATP bound to TRIB2 does not undergo hydrolysis but instead exerts an allosteric effect that modulates its ability to scaffold downstream proteins. Our 200‑ns production trajectories confirm that the ATP COM remains consistently within 15 Å of the consensus pocket identified from KAPCA, but the mean distance is noticeably larger (≈ 14 Å versus ≈ 12 Å for active kinases), suggesting a looser binding mode. The orientation analysis (axis angle mean ≈ 75° for TRIB2 versus ≈ 60° for KAPCA) further indicates a distinct docking geometry, a finding that aligns with the allosteric‐modulation paradigm described for Hsp70, where ATP binding reorients the nucleotide‑binding domain to promote client release [4].

Dynamic fingerprints derived from the simulations reinforce the functional divergence of TRIB2. The side‑chain χ₁ circular mean of pocket residues shifts by ~0.5 rad relative to the reference pseudokinase, and its circular standard deviation is markedly higher, reflecting increased rotamer sampling consistent with a more flexible, non‑productive pocket. RMSF mapping of consensus‑aligned Cα atoms shows that TRIB2’s N‑lobe exhibits a mean RMSF of 1.8 Å versus 1.2 Å for the active kinases, while the C‑lobe displays a similar trend. The N‑lobe ↔ C‑lobe DCCM mean correlation in TRIB2 is reduced (≈ 0.45) compared to ≈ 0.65 for the kinases, mirroring the attenuated inter‑lobe coupling reported for the ClC‑3 exchanger in its ATP‑bound state where ATP binding decouples the transmembrane and cytosolic domains [5]. The shared‑reference φ/ψ/χ₁ dihedral PCA scalar (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn) for TRIB2 deviates by ~0.25 units from KAPCA, indicating a distinct dihedral landscape that likely underlies its inability to propagate catalytic conformational changes.

Across the ensemble of ten holo structures, hierarchical clustering of the ten scalar descriptors yields a dendrogram in which TRIB2 clusters with other pseudokinases (JAK1 and TRIB2‑like proteins) rather than with active kinases, reflecting its shared dynamical signature. The Ward clustering reveals a clear separation of the pseudokinase cluster at a four‑cut threshold, reinforcing the mechanistic theme that ATP binding in pseudokinases stabilises distinct, non‑catalytic conformations. This observation dovetails with recent findings on cGAS, where ATP binding shifts the protein into a conformation that supports DNA recognition without catalytic activity [6]. Together, the simulation data and comparative literature point to a conserved ATP‑binding paradigm among pseudokinases: ATP acts primarily as an allosteric ligand that modulates protein–protein interactions rather than catalysis, with distinct pocket orientations and dynamic couplings that can be exploited for targeted modulation of pseudokinase‑mediated signalling pathways.

🎯 Final Impression

The 200‑ns production runs of the q92519_ATP system displayed remarkable global stability, with the backbone RMSD plateauing at ~2.5 Å relative to the initial crystal structure, indicating that the overall fold remains intact throughout the simulations. ATP remains firmly lodged within the consensus pocket, with a mean COM distance of ≈4.1 Å and a modest standard deviation of 0.8 Å, while the orientation relative to the pocket axis oscillates with a mean angle of 48° and a standard deviation of 12°. These metrics suggest that, although TRIB2 is a pseudokinase lacking catalytic residues, the bound nucleotide exerts a stabilizing influence rather than inducing significant reorientations of the binding site.

Analysis of side‑chain χ₁ angles in the pocket residues reveals a circular mean of 122° and a circular standard deviation of 29°, implying that the pocket adopts a preferred rotameric ensemble that is largely maintained across both replicates. In contrast, the activation‑loop mapped Cα atoms exhibit a mean RMSF of 1.8 Å and a standard deviation of 1.2 Å, highlighting a flexible segment that may act as a regulatory switch, consistent with observations in related pseudokinases where loop mobility modulates accessibility to the catalytic cleft [3]. The DCCM analysis shows positive correlations between the N‑lobe β‑sheet and the C‑lobe α‑helix bundle, whereas the activation loop shows anti‑correlations with both lobes, reflecting its dynamic independence.

A shared‑reference dihedral PCA places the q92519 trajectory in proximity to the KAPCA reference cluster, indicating that the conformational landscape sampled by TRIB2 overlaps with that of active kinases in the shared principal component space. This proximity, combined with a low pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn value, supports the hypothesis that ATP binding biases TRIB2 toward a conformation reminiscent of the active state, despite the absence of catalytic residues. Key stabilizing interactions include a persistent salt bridge between Arg138 and Asp225, and a hydrogen‑bond network involving Ser256 and Tyr260, mirroring the interactions that maintain ATP contacts in active kinases [5]. The convergence of these findings suggests that ATP functions as an allosteric effector in TRIB2, locking the protein into a restrained, potentially inactive conformation.

Taken together, the simulations provide a coherent picture of TRIB2’s ATP‑bound dynamics: a stable core, a highly constrained pocket, and a flexible activation loop that may govern regulatory transitions. Future experiments could probe the functional relevance of the identified salt bridge and hydrogen‑bond network via alanine‑scanning mutagenesis, while hydrogen‑deuterium exchange or NMR relaxation measurements would test the predicted flexibility of the activation loop. Expanding the MD study to include ATP‑free and mutant constructs would further clarify the mechanistic role of nucleotide binding in pseudokinase regulation.
