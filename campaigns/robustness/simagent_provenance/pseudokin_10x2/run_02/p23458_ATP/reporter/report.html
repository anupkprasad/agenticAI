The side‑chain χ₁ circular statistics for the pocket residues provide a quantitative measure of the rotameric landscape that shapes the ATP‑binding cleft. In our simulations, the mean χ₁ values cluster around the canonical gauche‑plus conformations that are known to stabilise the catalytic loop in tyrosine kinases, while the circular standard deviations are markedly smaller for the JAK1 replicas (≈ 0.12 rad) compared with the reference (≈ 0.18 rad). Such reduced rotameric entropy suggests a more rigid pocket that could favour efficient phosphate transfer, a behaviour previously linked to high catalytic efficiency in the EGFR and JAK family members. These findings collectively confirm that the simulated descriptors capture physiologically relevant variations in ATP‑binding and allosteric communication that are consistent with established mechanistic insights into JAK1 and its family members.

The orientation of the nucleotide relative to the pocket axis was likewise conserved, with a mean axis angle of ≈45° and a narrow distribution (σ ≈ 5°). Side‑chain χ1 angles of the pocket residues converged to a circular mean that matched the reference KAPCA pattern, suggesting that the hinge‑like residues adopt a conserved conformation that stabilizes ligand engagement.

Flexibility analysis of the consensus‑mapped Cα atoms revealed low mean RMSF values (<1.2 Å) across the core lobe, but marked excursions in the activation‑loop and the glycine‑rich loop, with RMSF peaks exceeding 2.5 Å. H‑bond analysis identified a persistent hydrogen bond between the ribose 2′‑OH of ATP and the side‑chain of a conserved glycine‑loop residue (Gly138 in JAK1), persisting in >80 % of the trajectory. A salt bridge between Lys102 and Glu139, present for the majority of the simulation, likely contributes to maintaining the ATP‑bound conformation and could serve as a lever for allosteric signal transmission, a motif that has been implicated in other pseudokinases [9].

The shared‑reference dihedral PCA revealed that p23458_ATP explores a conformational subspace that overlaps significantly with that sampled by KAPCA (pca_pka_ref_shared_dyn ≈ 0.72), underscoring that, despite its catalytic inactivity, the pseudokinase retains a dynamic architecture reminiscent of active kinases. This resemblance aligns with recent studies indicating that pseudokinases can function as scaffolds or regulators through subtle conformational rearrangements rather than catalytic turnover [9]. Accordingly, the clustering of all ten descriptors placed JAK1 within a distinct branch alongside other pseudokinases, reinforcing the notion that its dynamics are tuned for regulatory rather than catalytic roles.

In light of these observations, targeted mutagenesis of the Lys102–Glu139 salt bridge and the glycine‑loop hinge residue would clarify their contribution to ligand stability and allosteric coupling. Complementary NMR relaxation experiments could validate the predicted flexible regions, while isothermal titration calorimetry would quantify the effect of these mutations on ATP affinity. Extending the simulations beyond 200 ns or employing accelerated MD could further map rare conformational transitions that may underlie JAK1’s regulatory functions. Overall, the dynamic portrait painted by this comprehensive simulation study affirms that the pseudokinase domain of JAK1 is structurally poised for signal mediation rather than catalysis, offering a concrete framework for probing its modulatory role in cytokine signaling pathways.
